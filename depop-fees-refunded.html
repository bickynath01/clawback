What Depop's Official Policy Actually Says

Depop spells it out plainly in its help center: "If your sale is refunded, all fees (the selling fee, payments processing fees and boosting fees) will be reversed too. This happens automatically for transactions using Depop Payments." And the fee page confirms US, UK and AUS sellers pay no selling fee in 2026 — just the regional processing fee (US: 3.3% + $0.45 on item + shipping + tax). Sellers outside those countries paying through PayPal get the 10% selling fee back after filing a ticket with proof of the refund — PayPal's own processing cut stays with PayPal, separate from anything Depop controls.

This policy is newer than a lot of people realize, which is exactly why the old advice is still floating around. Older threads describe the pre-2026 setup with the 10% selling fee and the earlier Depop-Stripe-UK arrangement, where a processing charge really could stick around after a refund. Once Depop Payments rolled out and the selling fee was removed for UK/US/AUS sellers, the rule changed for the better — a lot of the internet just hasn't caught up yet. When in doubt, trust the current help page over an old thread.

Why this trips people up: the "Depop eats your fees on every refund" advice was accurate for an older payment setup and got repeated so widely it's still the default assumption in a lot of seller communities. It's worth actually checking your own Latest Transactions after a refund rather than trusting the old rule of thumb — the processing line reverses same-day under Depop Payments, and seeing it happen once is usually enough to retire the myth for good.

The Boosting Fee Gotcha (The One Most Sellers Miss)

There's a clause almost nobody quotes from the help page: "If your sale is refunded, all fees (the selling fee, payments processing fees and boosting fees) will be reversed too." That last part matters more than ever in 2026, because the US/UK boosting fee rose to 12% for new listings from 23 March 2026. If a buyer returns a boosted $70 jacket, that's an $8.40 boosting charge that comes back automatically on Depop Payments — genuinely more generous than most platforms, where ad spend rarely reverses on a refund.

The practical habit worth building: after refunding a boosted item, check Latest Transactions specifically for the boosting line. If it hasn't reversed within 48 hours, a quick support ticket with the transaction ID usually clears it up — it should happen automatically, but the occasional glitch is normal.

EXAMPLE · THE TWO-DAY BOOSTING LAGSay you boost a $70 jacket for a weekend and it sells, then the buyer returns it. The processing fee ($2.76) typically reverses within the day — but the boosting charge ($8.40) can occasionally take a couple of extra days to show as reversed. It does clear, but that gap can feel like a charge that's gone for good if you're not expecting it. Worth screenshotting the reversal once it appears, just to have the record.

Three Return Traps Sellers Don't See Coming

The fee math above covers the obvious stuff. But the traps that actually cost money on returns are smaller, weirder, and almost never about the fees.

SCENARIO · THE PAYPAL TICKET LOOP (OUTSIDE UK/US/AUS)Picture a seller based in Canada who sold a $60 sweater through PayPal. The buyer returns it, the refund goes out through PayPal — smooth. But the $6 Depop selling fee doesn't reverse automatically for PayPal sales. It only comes back after filing a support ticket with the account email, both @usernames, and a screenshot proving the refund completed. Skip the ticket (or lose the screenshot) and that $6 is simply gone. The habit: take the proof screenshot the same minute you refund, and file the ticket the same day.
SCENARIO · THE BUNDLE WEIGHT TRAPSay a buyer bundles three or four hoodies into one order, the app estimates the weight, and auto-generates a shipping label. Everything ships fine, the sale closes, and the seller moves on. Then the buyer returns the bundle — and the return label reflects the real weight, not the estimate. On a not-as-described return the seller typically absorbs that label cost, and a 3 lb return can run $12.83 or more on USPS Ground Advantage rates. The habit that saves sellers here is simple: weigh bundles before trusting any auto-estimate, and price the return risk into the listing.
SCENARIO · CROSS-BORDER EXCHANGE SLUMPInternational sales carry their own quiet cost. Say a US seller ships a $50 shirt to a buyer in Canada, and a couple of days later it comes back for a refund. In the 48 hours between the sale and the return processing, the currency exchange rate shifts slightly — and the seller ends up covering a small amount out of pocket just to fully clear the buyer's balance back to zero. Depop isn't charging this on purpose; it's simply what happens when a refund crosses currencies and the rate isn't locked at the original sale price.

Full Refund vs Partial Refund vs Return: Which Hurts Least?

Full refund (Depop Payments): the buyer gets everything back and every fee line reverses. The real loss is return shipping (if any) plus whatever value the item lost.

Partial refund: the buyer keeps the item and gets some money back — Depop's page confirms partials are agreed between buyer and seller and don't require a return. The official policy doesn't spell out proportional fee reversals here, so it's worth double-checking the transaction breakdown afterward. Partials are usually the cheapest way out: no label, no value drop, no relisting.

SCENARIO · THE LOCKED LABEL REFUND QUEUEHere's a timing trap that catches even careful sellers: a seller prints a real shipping label for a $65 order, has it ready to go, and the buyer cancels minutes before it makes it to the post office. The refund to the buyer processes instantly — that part's smooth. But the money already spent on the label itself doesn't just bounce back the same day. It drops into a manual carrier refund queue that can take up to 14 days to clear, because the carrier has to confirm the label was genuinely never used before releasing the funds. So for two weeks, that shipping cost is just gone from working cash flow, even though the sale itself was refunded same-day.

Return + refund: on a not-as-described dispute, US buyers get a prepaid return label; buyers outside the US arrange tracked shipping and Depop reimburses them up to $20/£20. Items need to go back within 7 days of the agreed return. The real exposure here is time and the item's condition on arrival — photographing everything before and after shipping is the cheapest insurance available.

Buyer's Fault vs Seller's Fault vs Depop Stepped In

Changed mind / doesn't fit: Depop Protection doesn't cover remorse. The official policy lists what's actually covered — lost and damaged items, incorrect sizes, wrong colors, counterfeits, severe damage, major undisclosed wear, incorrect items, missing bundle pieces. Fit, smell, and "the material looks different in photos" aren't on that list, so there's no obligation to refund. Choosing to anyway still gets every fee reversed on Depop Payments.

Not as described / never arrived: the buyer can raise an issue within 30 days. Sellers get 48 hours to respond with a solution — silence auto-escalates straight to Depop, whose decision is final, including refunding without a return. That auto-escalation window is a real risk worth setting a reminder for; a single missed notification can turn a $40 jacket into a total loss.

Depop stepped in: once escalated, the outcome follows Depop's own review. If the buyer's claim is upheld without a return, the item, the sale, and the return shipping are all gone — but the fees still come back. The calculator above helps model exactly that worst-case scenario before it happens.

SCENARIO · THE MISSED ESCALATION WINDOWSay a seller misses the 48-hour response window on a not-as-described claim, because the notification got buried under other alerts. The claim auto-escalates, Depop sides with the buyer, and an $85 jacket gets refunded without a return. The fees reverse — but the item, the sale, and any chance to contest it are gone. It's the kind of loss that's entirely preventable with one habit: setting a reminder the moment any "issue opened" notification comes in, since the clock is the real risk here, not the fees.

How To Refund A Buyer (Exact Steps, iOS + Android)

REFUND + FEE REVERSAL WALKTHROUGH

  1. Open the app. iOS: tap My Depop → Sold → All. Android: tap Selling Hub → Sold items.
  2. Select the order and tap Refund sale. For a partial, switch to the partial tab and enter the amount.
  3. Confirm. If the buyer paid with Depop Payments, the refund comes out of the Depop Balance — or the connected top-up card if the balance is too low.
  4. Verify the reversal. Go to My Depop → My Account → Depop Balance → scroll to Latest Transactions. The original fee lines should flip back within the same day.

PayPal-sold items get refunded through PayPal directly (desktop only). Sellers outside the UK/US/AUS then file a support ticket to bring the Depop selling fee back separately. Slower, but it works — just keep the screenshots handy.

How Long Does A Depop Refund Take?

Refunds through Depop Payments typically process within a few hours, with the fee reversals showing up in Latest Transactions the same day. Bank-to-bank transfers can take 1-3 business days for the buyer to actually see the funds. PayPal refunds usually process in minutes on PayPal's end but can take up to 5 business days to hit the buyer's card.

FAQ

Does Depop refund fees when I refund a buyer in 2026?
Yes. On Depop Payments every fee reverses automatically — the 3.3% + $0.45 processing fee and any 12% boosting fee for US sellers.
Is there still a 10% selling fee to refund?
Not for US/UK/AUS sellers — the selling fee was removed in 2026. Sellers elsewhere still pay 10%, refunded via a support ticket on PayPal sales.
What happens to boosting fees if I refund a buyer?
Boosting fees are reversed too — 12% in the US/UK (new listings from 23 Mar 2026), 8% elsewhere. Verify the boosting line in Latest Transactions.
Does Depop charge a refund fee?
No. Depop reverses the fees it already collected. Your real leftover cost is return shipping and any value drop in the item.
Do I get fees back on a partial refund on Depop?
Depop's help page confirms full-refund reversals but does not spell out partial refunds. Check the transaction breakdown in your Depop Balance afterward.
How do I get a Depop fee refund if I sold through PayPal?
Sellers outside the UK/US/AUS file a support ticket with their account email, both @usernames, and proof of the completed refund. PayPal's own processing fee is charged separately by PayPal.
How long does a Depop refund take to process?
Refunds issued through Depop Payments typically process within a few hours and the fee reversals appear in Latest Transactions the same day. Bank-to-bank transfers can take 1 to 3 business days for the buyer to see the funds.
What is a top-up card on Depop?
A top-up card is a connected card that lets you refund buyers even when your Depop Balance is too low. Depop requires you to add one before you can issue refunds.
Does refunding a buyer protect my seller standing?
Resolving issues quickly keeps disputes out of escalation. If you do not respond to a not-as-described issue within 48 hours it is automatically escalated to Depop, whose decision is final.
