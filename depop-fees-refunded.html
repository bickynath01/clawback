The short answer: If the buyer paid through Depop Payments, Depop hands back everything it took — the 10% selling fee, the payments processing fee, even boosting fees — automatically, the second the refund goes through. Sold via PayPal? The selling fee still comes back, but you'll need to raise a support ticket for it, and PayPal keeps its own processing fee regardless. Either way, the fees were never really the danger. The real damage is almost always the return shipping and whatever the item is worth once it comes back to you.

That's the short version, and it clears up something half the reseller forums still get backwards. Below, there's the receipts at three price points, the PayPal catch nobody explains well, a boosting-fee detail almost nobody mentions, exact steps to check the reversal yourself, and a few real-shaped scenarios that show where sellers actually lose money — which usually isn't where they expect.

The myth worth killing: a lot of reseller blogs still repeat that "the processing fee never comes back on Depop." That was true once, but it describes PayPal's fee behavior and other platforms' rules, not Depop Payments today (eBay, for comparison, keeps its $0.30 per-order fee no matter what — see does eBay refund the per-order fee?). Different platform, different rail, different rule. Worth knowing which one applies before assuming the worst.

What Depop's Official Policy Actually Says

Depop spells this out pretty plainly in its help center: "If your sale is refunded, all fees (the selling fee, payments processing fees and boosting fees) will be reversed too. This happens automatically for transactions using Depop Payments." Sellers outside the UK, US or Australia paying through PayPal get the selling fee back after filing a ticket with proof of the refund — PayPal's own processing cut stays with PayPal, separate from anything Depop controls.

This policy is newer than a lot of people realize, which is exactly why the old advice is still floating around. Older threads describe the earlier Depop-Stripe-UK setup, where that processing charge really did stick around after a refund. Once Depop Payments rolled out and things got standardized under eBay's ownership, the rule changed for the better — a lot of the internet just hasn't caught up yet. When in doubt, trust the current help page over an old thread.

Why this trips people up: the "Depop eats your processing fee on every refund" advice was accurate for the old payment setup and got repeated so widely it's still the default assumption in a lot of seller communities. It's worth actually checking your own Latest Transactions after a refund rather than trusting the old rule of thumb — the processing line reverses same-day under Depop Payments, and seeing it happen once is usually enough to retire the myth for good.

The Boosting Fee Gotcha (The One Most Sellers Miss)

There's a clause almost nobody quotes from the help page: "If your sale is refunded, all fees (the selling fee, payments processing fees and boosting fees) will be reversed too." That last part matters more than it looks. Boosted items carry an extra 8% on top of the selling fee, and if a buyer returns something that was boosted, that 8% comes back automatically on Depop Payments too. That's genuinely more generous than most platforms — eBay's Promoted Listings, Poshmark, and Mercari don't fully reverse ad spend on a refund the same way.

The practical habit worth building: after refunding a boosted item, check Latest Transactions specifically for the boosting line. If it hasn't reversed within 48 hours, a quick support ticket with the transaction ID usually clears it up — it should happen automatically, but the occasional glitch is normal.

EXAMPLE · THE THREE-DAY BOOSTING LAGSay you boost a $70 jacket for a weekend and it sells, then the buyer returns it. The 10% selling fee and the 2.9% processing fee typically reverse within the day — but the boosting charge (around $5.60 here) can occasionally take a couple of extra days to show as reversed. It does clear, but that gap can feel like a charge that's gone for good if you're not expecting it. Worth screenshotting the reversal once it appears, just to have the record.

Three More Refund Traps Sellers Don't See Coming

The fee math above covers the obvious stuff. But a handful of smaller, weirder traps show up often enough to be worth knowing about before they hit an account.

SCENARIO · INSTANT CANCELLATIONSPicture a buyer grabbing a $35 listing and cancelling it five minutes later, before it's even been packed. The refund itself is instant and painless — one tap and it's done. But if that sale ran through Depop Payments, the $0.45 processing charge doesn't come back with it, because that fee was already paid out to the card network the moment the sale happened. It's such a small number that most sellers never notice it, but it's real and it's non-refundable. Do the math on a handful of fast cancellations in a slow week and it adds up to a few dollars quietly gone for sales that technically never even shipped. Nobody's being cheated here — it's just how payment processing works — but it's worth knowing so a string of instant cancellations doesn't feel mysterious.
SCENARIO · THE BUNDLE WEIGHT TRAPSay a buyer bundles three or four hoodies into one order, the app estimates the weight, and auto-generates a cheap $5.99 shipping label. Everything ships fine, the sale closes, and the seller moves on. Then, two weeks later, a surprise $7.10 charge shows up out of nowhere — because a USPS scale flagged the actual package as heavier than what the label paid for. This isn't Depop being sneaky; it's standard postal weight verification, and it can land weeks after a sale feels finished and forgotten. The habit that saves sellers here is simple: weigh anything over a couple pounds themselves before trusting the app's auto-estimate on bundles, since that retroactive charge can land straight on a card or get pulled from the next payout.
SCENARIO · CROSS-BORDER EXCHANGE SLUMPInternational sales carry their own quiet cost too. Say a US seller ships a $50 shirt to a buyer in Canada, and a couple of days later it comes back for a refund. In the 48 hours between the sale and the return processing, the currency exchange rate shifts slightly — and the seller ends up covering an extra $1.15 out of pocket just to fully clear the buyer's balance back to zero. Depop isn't charging this on purpose; it's simply what happens when a refund crosses currencies and the rate isn't locked at the original sale price. It's a small number most months, but sellers who do a lot of cross-border business learn to expect it as part of the territory.

Full Refund vs Partial Refund vs Return: Which Hurts Least?

Full refund (Depop Payments): the buyer gets everything back and every fee line reverses. The real loss is return shipping (if any) plus whatever value the item lost.

Partial refund: the buyer keeps the item and gets some money back — Depop's page confirms partials are agreed between buyer and seller and don't require a return. The official policy doesn't spell out proportional fee reversals here, so it's worth double-checking the transaction breakdown afterward. Partials are usually the cheapest way out: no label, no value drop, no relisting.

SCENARIO · THE LOCKED LABEL REFUND QUEUEHere's a timing trap that catches even careful sellers: a seller prints a real shipping label for a $65 order, has it ready to go, and the buyer cancels minutes before it makes it to the post office. The refund to the buyer processes instantly — that part's smooth. But the money already spent on the label itself doesn't just bounce back the same day. It drops into a manual carrier refund queue that can take up to 14 days to clear, because the carrier has to confirm the label was genuinely never used before releasing the funds. So for two weeks, that shipping cost is just gone from working cash flow, even though the sale itself was refunded same-day. Nothing about it is technically broken — it's just slow, and nobody warns sellers about the wait upfront.

Return + refund: on a not-as-described dispute, US buyers get a prepaid return label; buyers outside the US arrange tracked shipping and Depop reimburses them up to $20/£20. Items need to go back within 7 days of the agreed return. The real exposure here is time and the item's condition on arrival — photographing everything before and after shipping is the cheapest insurance available.

Buyer's Fault vs Seller's Fault vs Depop Stepped In

Changed mind / doesn't fit: Depop Protection doesn't cover remorse. The official policy lists what's actually covered — lost and damaged items, incorrect sizes, wrong colors, counterfeits, severe damage, major undisclosed wear, incorrect items, missing bundle pieces. Fit, smell, and "the material looks different in photos" aren't on that list, so there's no obligation to refund. Choosing to anyway still gets fees reversed on Depop Payments.

Not as described / never arrived: the buyer can raise an issue within 30 days. Sellers get 48 hours to respond with a solution — silence auto-escalates straight to Depop, whose decision is final, including refunding without a return. That auto-escalation window is a real risk worth setting a reminder for; a single missed notification can turn a $40 jacket into a total loss.

Depop stepped in: once escalated, the outcome follows Depop's own review. If the buyer's claim is upheld without a return, the item, the sale, and the return shipping are all gone — but the fees still come back. The calculator above helps model exactly that worst-case scenario before it happens.

SCENARIO · THE MISSED ESCALATION WINDOWSay a seller misses the 48-hour response window on a not-as-described claim, because the notification got buried under other alerts. The claim auto-escalates, Depop sides with the buyer, and an $85 jacket gets refunded without a return. The fees reverse — but the item, the sale, and any chance to contest it are gone. It's the kind of loss that's entirely preventable with one habit: setting a reminder the moment any "issue opened" notification comes in, since the clock is the real risk here, not the fees.

How To Refund A Buyer (Exact Steps, iOS + Android)

REFUND + FEE REVERSAL WALKTHROUGH

  1. Open the app. iOS: tap My Depop → Sold → All. Android: tap Selling Hub → Sold items.
  2. Select the order and tap Refund sale. For a partial, switch to the partial tab and enter the amount.
  3. Confirm. If the buyer paid with Depop Payments, the refund comes out of the Depop Balance — or the connected top-up card if the balance is too low.
  4. Verify the reversal. Go to My Depop → My Account → Depop Balance → scroll to Latest Transactions. The original fee lines should flip back within the same day.

PayPal-sold items get refunded through PayPal directly (desktop only), then a support ticket brings the Depop selling fee back separately. Slower, but it works — just keep the screenshots handy.

How Long Does A Depop Refund Take?

Refunds through Depop Payments typically process within a few hours, with the fee reversals showing up in Latest Transactions the same day. Bank-to-bank transfers can take 1-3 business days for the buyer to actually see the funds. PayPal refunds usually process in minutes on PayPal's end but can take up to 5 business days to hit the buyer's card.

The "when will my money actually show up" anxiety is real on both sides of a transaction — the full payout timeline, including the new-seller reserve, is broken down in when do I get paid on Depop? A refund doesn't extend the payout window, but an open dispute does freeze funds until it's resolved.

Conclusion: Price The Risk, Not The Myth

The 10% commission, the processing fee, the boosting fee — on Depop Payments, they all come back on a refund. What doesn't come back is the return label, whatever value the item lost, and the 48-hour response window if a dispute opens. That's the real math worth building into pricing, not the outdated "processing fee never comes back" myth that's cost sellers money for longer than it should have.

FAQ

Does Depop refund the 10% selling fee when I refund a buyer?
Yes. Depop's official policy states that when a sale is refunded, the selling fee is reversed. With Depop Payments this happens automatically when the refund processes.
Does Depop refund the payment processing fee too?
With Depop Payments, yes — the payments processing fee is reversed along with the selling fee and boosting fees. With PayPal, only the Depop selling fee is refunded via a support ticket; PayPal's own processing fee usually stays.
Do I get fees back on a partial refund on Depop?
Depop's help page confirms full-refund reversals but does not spell out partial refunds. Check the transaction breakdown in your Depop Balance after issuing a partial refund, since partials do not require the buyer to return the item.
How do I get a Depop fee refund if I sold through PayPal?
Fill out Depop's contact form with your account email, your @username, the buyer's @username, and proof of the completed refund. Depop will refund the selling fee; the PayPal processing fee is charged separately by PayPal.
What happens to boosting fees if I refund a buyer?
Boosting fees are reversed too. Depop's policy lists the selling fee, payments processing fees and boosting fees as all being reversed when a sale is refunded. Verify the specific boosting line in Latest Transactions.
Does Depop charge a refund fee?
No. Depop does not charge a separate refund fee. It simply reverses the fees it already collected on the original sale. Your real leftover cost is return shipping and any value drop in the item.
How long does a Depop refund take to process?
Refunds issued through Depop Payments typically process within a few hours and the fee reversals appear in Latest Transactions the same day. Bank-to-bank transfers can take 1 to 3 business days for the buyer to see the funds.
What is a top-up card on Depop?
A top-up card is a connected card that lets you refund buyers even when your Depop Balance is too low. Depop requires you to add one before you can issue refunds.
Does refunding a buyer protect my seller standing?
Resolving issues quickly keeps disputes out of escalation. If you do not respond to a not-as-described issue within 48 hours it is automatically escalated to Depop, whose decision is final.
