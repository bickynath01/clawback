Are Depop Fees Refunded When You Refund a Buyer? (2026)

If you just refunded a Depop buyer, there is one question that usually comes first: "Did I just lose the Depop fees too?"

For Depop Payments, the current answer is reassuring: when a sale is refunded, Depop says the applicable selling, payment-processing, and boosting fees are reversed. For sellers using PayPal outside the UK, US, or Australia, the Depop selling fee requires a Support request instead.

But there is an important catch: getting your Depop fees reversed does not mean every cost connected to the refund disappears. Shipping, return costs, packaging, and any loss in the item's value are separate. That distinction is what matters if you're trying to work out what the refund actually cost you.

LAST REVIEWED: SEPTEMBER 2026

The short answer

If the buyer paid with Depop Payments

Depop currently says that when the sale is refunded:

  • the applicable selling fee is reversed
  • the payment-processing fee is reversed
  • the boosting fee, if applicable, is reversed

The reversal happens automatically as part of the refund process.

If the sale used PayPal

For sellers outside the UK, US, and Australia, Depop says you can get the Depop selling fee refunded by contacting Support and providing proof of the completed refund. PayPal's own processing fees are a separate matter and are governed by PayPal's rules.

So the answer isn't simply:

"Depop refunds all fees."

The better answer is:

Depop's current policy reverses the applicable Depop fees when a sale is refunded, but the process depends on the payment method and other seller costs are separate.

Depop's current seller-fee policy explicitly says that if a sale is refunded, all fees — selling, payment processing, and boosting — are reversed. Depop's Boosted Listings policy says the same for boosting fees. That means you should not automatically treat the original fee deduction as a permanent loss.

Does Depop refund the payment-processing fee?

Yes, for Depop Payments. Depop's current seller-fee policy specifically includes payment-processing fees among the fees reversed when a sale is refunded.

So if you sold an item through Depop Payments, paid the applicable processing fee, and later issued a refund, you should check the updated transaction rather than assuming that processing fee is gone forever. This matters because older articles and forum answers can give a different answer. Depop's current policy is the source to use for the current transaction.

Does Depop refund the 10% selling fee?

Yes, where that selling fee actually applies. There is an important 2026 detail here:

Sellers in the UK, US, and Australia currently have no Depop selling fee. Sellers outside those markets can still have a 10% Depop selling fee under the applicable fee rules.

So if you're a US seller asking "Will Depop refund my 10% fee?" you may be looking at an older fee structure. Your current transaction may have no Depop selling fee at all, although payment-processing and boosting fees can still apply. That is why your country and actual sale receipt matter more than an old "Depop takes 10%" rule.

Does Depop refund the boosting fee?

Yes. Depop's current Boosted Listings Policy specifically says that if the buyer is refunded, boosting fees, selling fees, and payment-processing fees are refunded to the seller.

That means a boosted sale should not automatically become more expensive simply because you had to refund the buyer. If you are wondering "I refunded a boosted Depop sale — did I lose the boost fee?" check the updated transaction rather than assuming the fee stayed with Depop.

The part sellers often miss: fee refund ≠ total-loss refund

This is the most important distinction on the page. Suppose you sell an item and later refund the buyer. There are two separate financial questions.

Question 1: What did Depop charge me?

  • selling fee, where applicable
  • payment-processing fee
  • boosting fee, where applicable

For a refunded Depop Payments sale, those applicable Depop fees are reversed under the current policy.

Question 2: What other money did I spend?

  • shipping and return shipping
  • packaging and supplies
  • other seller-paid expenses
  • loss in the item's recoverable value

Those aren't automatically transformed into Depop fee refunds. So a refund can still be a real loss for a seller even when the platform fees come back.

Example: a $100 Depop sale gets fully refunded

Imagine you sell an item for $100 through Depop Payments. You paid a payment-processing fee and also boosted the listing. Later, the buyer receives a full refund. Your first instinct might be: "I lost the $100 sale plus all those fees." That's not necessarily the final position. Under Depop's current policy, the applicable Depop fees are reversed when the sale is refunded. The remaining financial impact might instead come from something like:

  • $100 returned to the buyer
  • Depop fees reversed
  • $8 return shipping paid by you
  • $3 packaging already used
  • $15 reduction in resale value because the item came back damaged

In that simplified example, the platform fees are not the main loss. The seller's remaining exposure comes from the other costs and item-value change. That's the number CLAWBACK is interested in.

I refunded the buyer. Why does my balance look wrong?

A refund can make your Depop Balance look very different from the original sale. For Depop Payments, Depop says the refund is taken from your available Depop Balance. If the balance isn't enough, a connected top-up card may be charged to fund the refund. So don't judge the final result from the original payout alone. Look at the updated transaction.

Original sale→Buyer refund→Fee reversal→Updated Depop Balance

That final position is what you should use when reconciling the transaction.

How do I check whether Depop refunded my fees?

If you used Depop Payments, check the transaction inside Depop. Depop's current guidance says sellers can view their activity through:

My Depop → My Account → Depop Balance → Latest Transactions

You can then review the transaction and its fee breakdown. Look for: the original sale, the refund, the fee reversal, any other adjustment, and the resulting balance. If you're comparing the numbers manually, don't compare only the original sale receipt with the amount refunded to the buyer. The post-refund transaction is the important one.

What if I don't see the fee refund?

First, make sure the buyer's refund actually processed. Then check the updated transaction.

If you used Depop Payments

Depop says the applicable fees are automatically refunded when the refund is processed. If the refund has processed but the expected fee reversal is missing, contact Depop Support with the transaction details.

If you used PayPal outside the UK, US, or Australia

This is different. Depop says you need to contact Support to request the Depop selling-fee refund. Depop asks for: the email address linked to your Depop account, your Depop @username, your buyer's @username, and proof of the completed refund. Depop's current help page specifically asks for a screenshot showing the completed PayPal refund.

What if I only gave a partial refund?

A partial refund is different from a full refund. Depop defines a partial refund as a situation where the buyer keeps the item but receives some money back. Partial refunds can be agreed between the buyer and seller and don't require the item to be returned.

This is where I would avoid giving you a made-up formula such as:

"You'll always get exactly X% of your fees back."

The current Depop documentation confirms the refund mechanism, but the safest way to determine the actual financial result of a particular partial refund is to check the resulting transaction. Depop's Support request flow also specifically handles partial-refund cases and notes that, for Depop Payments, the Depop fee refund is processed once the partial refund has been processed.

Seller rule of thumb: for a partial refund, check the resulting transaction rather than guessing from the original fee percentage. That keeps your calculation tied to what Depop actually recorded.

What about shipping?

Shipping is where the phrase "all fees are refunded" can become misleading if you read it too broadly. A shipping cost isn't necessarily a Depop selling fee. For example, you might have: paid for a shipping label, paid return shipping, used packaging, offered free shipping, or had a seller-paid shipping expense. Those costs need to be considered separately. Depop's API documentation also separates seller refund components into Depop fees, payment fees, advertising fees, and shipping-related refunds, which reinforces that these are different parts of the transaction.

Fee reversal ≠ every seller expense automatically recovered.

What if the buyer returns the item damaged?

This is where a refund can still create a meaningful seller loss. Imagine: Depop fees are fully reversed, the buyer receives the refund, the item comes back — and the item is now worth $30 less than before. The Depop fee refund doesn't compensate you for that $30 change in item value.

That's why a seller trying to understand the true cost of a return needs more than a fee calculator. You need to separate platform fees recovered from money and value that actually remain lost.

What if Depop refunded the buyer without the item coming back?

This is another reason not to calculate your loss simply from the original fee deduction. If the buyer receives a refund and you don't recover the item, the item itself can become part of your exposure.

Depop's current buyer-protection guidance includes situations such as items not received, items not as described, counterfeit items, severe damage, incorrect items, and missing items in bundles. In some cases Depop may provide a full or partial refund without requiring a return. If that happens, the seller's question changes from "Did Depop refund my fee?" to "How much of the transaction did I actually recover?" That is a much more useful number.

What if Depop refunded the buyer automatically?

If Depop resolves a buyer issue on your behalf, don't assume the financial result is identical to a voluntary refund you issued yourself. Depop's seller Resolution Centre guidance says that when a seller resolves an issue by refunding the buyer, the applicable Depop selling fee is automatically refunded once the refund has processed. Depop can also resolve disputes itself under its terms.

If you didn't initiate the refund yourself, check: why the refund happened, whether the item is being returned, whether you received the item back, the final transaction adjustment, and your current Depop Balance.

A simple way to reconcile a refunded Depop sale

If you're trying to work out what happened to your money, don't start with a calculator. Start with the transaction.

  1. Find the original sale. Record: item price, shipping, applicable fees, and the amount you actually received.
  2. Find the refund. Record: amount returned to the buyer, whether it was full or partial, and whether the item was returned.
  3. Find the fee reversal. Check whether the applicable Depop fees were reversed.
  4. Check the physical item. If it came back: is it still sellable, is it damaged, has its value changed? If it didn't come back: enter the recoverable value as $0 unless you actually recovered something.
  5. Add seller-paid costs. Include only expenses you actually paid — return shipping, seller-paid label, other documented expenses.
  6. Calculate the real loss. Seller loss = refund exposure + seller-paid expenses − recovered value − confirmed reimbursements. Do not automatically add the original Depop fees again when the transaction already shows those fees were reversed. That's how you avoid double-counting.

Why older Depop fee-refund answers can be confusing

If you've searched this question before, you may have found answers that don't agree. That's because Depop's fee structure has changed. For example, sellers in the UK, US, and Australia currently have no Depop selling fee, while payment-processing and boosting fees can still apply. Sellers in other locations can have a 10% selling fee under the applicable rules.

So an article saying "Depop takes 10% from every seller" isn't a safe universal answer for 2026. Likewise, older discussions about processing fees may describe an earlier refund process. When you're reconciling a current sale, use the current Depop policy and the actual transaction receipt.

What sellers actually need to know

The good news is simple:

A refunded Depop sale does not automatically mean you permanently lose the original Depop fees. For Depop Payments, Depop currently says the applicable selling, processing, and boosting fees are reversed when the sale is refunded.

But don't stop your calculation there. A refund can still leave you with: shipping costs, return costs, packaging expenses, a damaged or lower-value item, an item that never comes back, and other seller-paid expenses. That's why "fees refunded" and "no loss" are not the same statement.

Frequently asked questions

Does Depop refund fees when you refund a buyer?

Yes. For Depop Payments, Depop's current policy says the applicable selling, payment-processing, and boosting fees are reversed when the sale is refunded. For PayPal sellers outside the UK, US, and Australia, the Depop selling-fee refund requires a Support request.

Does Depop refund the payment-processing fee?

Yes, for Depop Payments. Depop's current seller-fee policy explicitly includes payment-processing fees in the reversal when a sale is refunded.

Does Depop refund the 10% selling fee?

Yes, where the 10% selling fee applies. UK, US, and Australian sellers currently have no Depop selling fee, so check your country and transaction receipt before assuming a 10% fee was charged.

Does Depop refund boosting fees?

Yes. Depop's current Boosted Listings Policy says boosting fees are refunded when the buyer is refunded.

I refunded my buyer. Do I need to request my Depop fee back?

If the transaction used Depop Payments, the applicable fees are refunded automatically when the refund is processed. PayPal sellers outside the UK, US, and Australia need to contact Depop Support to request the Depop selling-fee refund.

Does a partial refund also reverse fees?

Depop supports partial refunds and its Support flow specifically addresses fee refunds after a partial refund. Because the actual transaction can determine the final adjustment, check the completed transaction rather than assuming a fixed dollar-for-dollar fee reversal.

Why does my Depop Balance look lower after a refund?

The buyer's refund is funded from your available Depop Balance or, where necessary, a connected top-up card. The fee reversal is a separate part of the transaction adjustment. Check the updated transaction rather than comparing only the original payout.

Does Depop refund shipping costs too?

Don't treat shipping as automatically included in the phrase "fee refund." Shipping and return-label costs are separate transaction components and can depend on the shipping arrangement.

What if Depop refunded the buyer but I didn't get the item back?

Then your actual loss can be larger than the platform-fee portion. Include the value of anything you did not recover when calculating your seller loss.

What should I do if the fee refund is missing?

Check the refunded transaction and your Depop Balance first. If the refund has processed but the expected fee adjustment is missing, contact Depop Support with the transaction details. PayPal sellers should include the completed refund proof requested by Depop.

The bottom line

If you just refunded a Depop buyer, don't count the original Depop fees as a permanent loss before checking the updated transaction. For Depop Payments, the current policy says the applicable selling, processing, and boosting fees are reversed when the sale is refunded.

The part that can still hurt is everything outside those platform fees: shipping + return costs + seller expenses + lost item value + anything you didn't recover.

So there are really two numbers worth knowing:

  • Fee recovered — what Depop reversed.
  • Actual seller loss — what remained after the refund, expenses, recovery, and any confirmed reimbursement.
