How Much Does Poshmark Take? Poshmark Fees Explained (2026)

If you sell on Poshmark, the number you usually notice first is 20%. But that number alone doesn't answer the question sellers actually care about:

"If I sell this item for $X, how much money will actually hit my Poshmark balance?"

That's where Poshmark gets more interesting. A $12 sale has a very different fee impact from a $60 sale. A bundle can create a completely different shipping calculation. An offer changes the final order price. A package over 5 pounds can create an additional seller shipping charge. And the buyer can see their own checkout charges that do not come out of your seller payout.

This guide breaks down the current U.S. Poshmark seller fees, the shipping costs sellers actually need to watch, the situations that make a 20% fee more or less painful, and the questions sellers keep running into in real-world sales.

The short answer: how much does Poshmark take?

For U.S. sellers, Poshmark's current seller fee is:

Sale pricePoshmark seller feeSeller keeps before other adjustments
Under $15$2.95 flatSale price − $2.95
$15 or more20%80% of the final order price

Poshmark says it is free to list an item. The seller fee is deducted after the item sells. Poshmark also notes that actual earnings can vary based on the final order price, seller discounts, fees, applicable taxes and other adjustments.

So the simple examples are:

  • $10 sale → $7.05 before other adjustments
  • $12 sale → $9.05
  • $15 sale → $12.00
  • $50 sale → $40.00
  • $100 sale → $80.00

That's the basic seller-fee math. But it isn't the whole economics of a Poshmark sale.

The first Poshmark fee mistake: using the listing price instead of the final sale price

You might list an item for $80. But then a buyer sends an offer for $60. Your seller fee is based on the final order price, not the original $80 listing price.

At $60: $60 × 20% = $12 fee, leaving $60 − $12 = $48.

That's why your listing price, offer price and actual payout should never be treated as the same number. Poshmark explicitly says estimated earnings can vary based on the final order price and seller discounts.

Seller rule: Calculate your margin from the price you actually expect to sell for — not the price you typed into the listing box.

What does Poshmark take from a $100 sale?

Sale price: $100. Poshmark fee: 20% = $20. Seller amount remaining: $80.

You keep $80 from a $100 sale.

The buyer's shipping charge is not another $6.49 deducted from that $80. Standard Poshmark shipping is paid by the buyer. Poshmark currently uses a $6.49 flat-rate USPS Ground Advantage label for packages up to 5 pounds in the U.S.

Sellers sometimes look at the buyer's checkout total and assume all of those charges affect the seller payout. They don't.

What about a sale under $15?

This is where the percentage can look surprisingly high. Suppose you sell something for $5. Poshmark takes $2.95. You keep $2.05 — an effective seller-fee rate of 59% of the sale price.

The flat $2.95 fee matters much more on cheap individual sales. That's why low-dollar inventory deserves a different pricing strategy from $50–$100 inventory — and recent seller discussions include sellers saying they avoid very low-priced individual sales because the economics don't work for them.

Does Poshmark charge a separate payment-processing fee?

For the normal U.S. seller fee structure, you don't add a separate credit-card processing percentage on top of the 20% seller fee. Poshmark describes its seller fee as covering the transaction processing and other services associated with the sale.

Don't calculate "20% Poshmark fee + another 3% payment-processing fee" for an ordinary Poshmark sale. That would double-count the fee.

Does Poshmark take 20% of the buyer's $6.49 shipping?

For the standard U.S. setup, the buyer pays the standard shipping charge separately. The $6.49 shipping charge is not another 20% seller deduction from your item price.

So for a normal $100 sale under the standard shipping limit: item sale $100, seller fee −$20, seller amount $80, buyer shipping a separate buyer charge.

The 5-pound number that can change your seller economics

The standard Poshmark shipping label covers packages up to 5 pounds — the total package being shipped, not simply the clothing item's advertised weight. If your bundle or package is heavier, you need a heavier label. And this changed in 2026 with a simplified overweight structure:

Package weightLabelSeller-paid upgrade
Up to 5 lbsStandard label$0
5.1–10 lbsUpgraded label$5
10.1–15 lbsUpgraded label$10

Poshmark says the seller pays the difference for the heavier label.

Example

A buyer purchases a bundle for $60. Seller fee: $12. The package weighs 7 lbs, so the overweight upgrade is $5. Seller amount before other costs: $60 − $12 − $5 = $43 — very different from simply doing $60 × 80% = $48. The shipping weight created the additional $5 seller cost.

Why bundles can surprise sellers

Bundles are attractive because multiple items can ship together under the same standard shipping charge when the package remains within the supported weight. That's useful — until the combined package crosses the threshold.

Imagine jeans (2 lbs) + shoes (3 lbs) + sweater (2 lbs) = a 7 lbs package. The buyer doesn't simply get another standard label for the same package. You need to account for the heavier shipment.

Before accepting a large bundle, ask: "What will the final package actually weigh?" Not: "What does each individual listing weigh?"

The hidden Poshmark fee problem: discounts

A seller can list something at $80 and still receive substantially less than $64 if other seller-side adjustments apply, because sellers can accept buyer offers, send offers, apply bundle discounts, participate in promotions, or offer shipping discounts in eligible situations.

The fee calculation should start from the actual transaction, not the original listing price. Poshmark itself warns that estimated earnings can change based on the final order price, seller discounts, fees and applicable taxes/discounts.

Expected selling price→seller fee→applicable seller costs→sourcing cost→actual profit

…rather than: listing price − 20%.

Why your Poshmark payout can feel "lower than 80%"

Sellers often compare: "I sold it for $50. Why didn't I get exactly $40?" The first thing to check is the actual final order price. Then check for seller discounts, bundle discounts, shipping-label upgrades, other applicable fees or adjustments, and taxes or governmental charges that may apply.

The right troubleshooting order

  1. What was the final sale price?
  2. What seller fee was deducted?
  3. Was a shipping upgrade required?
  4. Was a seller discount involved?
  5. Are there any other line items in the transaction record?

Don't start by assuming Poshmark "took more than 20%." Reconcile the transaction first.

What is Posh Protect and does it change my 20% fee?

Posh Protect is Poshmark's built-in protection system. It isn't a second percentage deducted from your seller payout. Current rules allow buyers to open qualifying cases within 72 hours of delivery for issues including undisclosed damage, incorrect or missing item, item not as described, and authenticity issues. The buyer must provide supporting photos. If Poshmark approves the return, Poshmark provides a return label.

For sellers, this matters because a return can create a separate problem if the item comes back damaged or materially different from the condition in which it was shipped. That's why we built a separate guide for that: Poshmark Damaged Returns: What Sellers Lose When an Item Comes Back Worse.

Seel Worry-Free Returns are different

Poshmark has also been testing Worry-Free Returns powered by Seel for U.S. sellers. This is not the same thing as Posh Protect. Poshmark announced in January 2026 that the program was being tested as an optional buyer-paid add-on: buyers can receive additional return flexibility (including fit or change of mind), while sellers' earnings and payout timing are not affected and returned items go to Seel rather than the seller.

Posh Protect: Poshmark's standard protection and qualifying return process.
Seel Worry-Free Returns: an optional buyer-paid program being tested for eligible U.S. transactions.
Don't write "Poshmark now accepts seven-day returns" — that's too broad.

The $20 sale that looks fine until you calculate the whole business

Sell an item for $20: Poshmark fee $4, you keep $16. But suppose you bought the item for $6 — your gross margin before supplies and operating costs is $10. If you also absorb a seller-side shipping upgrade or another applicable cost, that margin gets smaller.

The useful question is: "What do I actually make after Poshmark takes its fee and I account for my own costs?"

Your Poshmark fee is not your profit

LineAmount
Sale price$50.00
Poshmark seller fee−$10.00
Seller payout before other adjustments$40.00
Your sourcing cost−$12.00
Packaging / operating costs−$2.00
Approximate profit$26.00

That's why two sellers can both sell an item for $50 and have completely different profits. The platform fee is only one line.

A better way to price Poshmark items

Instead of asking "What should I list this for?", start with "What do I need to keep?" For sales of $15 or more, because you keep approximately 80% after the standard seller fee:

  • Keep $40 → $40 ÷ 0.80 = $50 final sale
  • Keep $60 → $60 ÷ 0.80 = $75
  • Keep $80 → $80 ÷ 0.80 = $100

This is much more useful than simply thinking "Poshmark takes 20%."

What Reddit sellers keep running into

  1. "The fee is painful on low-dollar sales."
    Recent sellers discuss avoiding very low-price items because the flat $2.95 fee consumes a large percentage of the sale.
  2. "Why does my payout look different from my listing price?"
    The answer often starts with the final transaction price, discounts and other applicable adjustments rather than the original listing price. Poshmark itself warns that estimated earnings can vary.
  3. "Why did my bundle become more expensive?"
    Weight. The standard label covers up to 5 lbs, while heavier packages require a heavier label. Poshmark simplified those upgrade tiers in 2026.
  4. "Are buyers seeing extra fees?"
    Buyers may still see shipping and applicable taxes at checkout, but those buyer-side charges are separate from the seller fee and are not deducted from your seller payout.
  5. "Does a Poshmark sale really leave me with 80%?"
    For a $15+ sale the standard seller fee is 20%, but the actual transaction can differ from a simple 80% calculation because Poshmark says estimated earnings vary with final order price, seller discounts, fees, taxes and other applicable adjustments.

Poshmark fees: the seller checklist

Before accepting an offer, check:

  • Final offer price
  • Expected Poshmark seller fee
  • Your sourcing cost
  • Bundle discount, if applicable
  • Shipping weight
  • Whether the package could exceed 5 lbs
  • Any seller-paid shipping promotion or adjustment
  • Expected profit after your own costs

For bundles:

  • Add the item weights
  • Include packaging
  • Check the combined package weight
  • Account for the heavier-label tier if necessary

For low-priced items:

  • Compare the $2.95 flat fee with your sale price
  • Consider whether bundling makes more economic sense

Poshmark fee myths worth ignoring

  • "Poshmark takes 20% plus another 3% processing fee." Not as a standard separate seller charge. Don't double-count.
  • "The buyer's $6.49 shipping comes out of my payout." No. Standard shipping is paid by the buyer.
  • "Every $50 listing means I receive $40." Not necessarily. $40 is the basic 80% calculation on a $50 final sale, before other applicable seller-side adjustments.
  • "A bundle is automatically cheaper to ship for the seller." The standard label can cover multiple items up to 5 lbs, but the combined package weight matters.
  • "The original listing price determines my fee." The transaction's final order price and applicable adjustments matter.

Poshmark fees vs. your actual profit

Sell an item for $75: Poshmark fee $15, seller amount $60. With cost of goods $20 and packaging/other direct costs $3, approximate profit is $37. Now imagine another seller sells the same $75 item but sourced it for $45 — their approximate profit is only $12. Same Poshmark fee. Completely different business result.

That's why CLAWBACK is built around the number you actually keep, not just the platform's advertised percentage.

The Poshmark fee rule that actually matters

Don't memorize "Poshmark takes 20%." Memorize this instead:

Your final sale price is only the starting point.

Check the final transaction price. Check the seller fee. Check the package weight. Check for discounts. Check for other applicable adjustments. Then calculate what you actually keep. That's the number that determines whether the sale made sense.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Poshmark take from a sale?

For U.S. sellers, Poshmark takes 20% of the final order price on sales of $15 or more, and a flat $2.95 on sales under $15. No separate payment-processing percentage is added on top of the standard seller fee.

What does Poshmark take from a $100 sale?

$20 (20%), leaving $80 before other applicable adjustments. Buyer-paid standard shipping is a separate buyer charge and is not deducted from the seller payout.

Does Poshmark charge a separate payment-processing fee?

No. The standard seller fee covers transaction processing and related services. Do not add another processing percentage on top of the 20% or the $2.95 flat fee.

Who pays Poshmark shipping?

The buyer pays the standard shipping charge ($6.49 USPS Ground Advantage for packages up to 5 lbs). If the package is heavier, the seller pays the overweight label upgrade: $5 for 5.1–10 lbs and $10 for 10.1–15 lbs under the 2026 simplified structure.

What is Posh Protect?

Poshmark's built-in protection system. Buyers can open qualifying cases within 72 hours of delivery for undisclosed damage, incorrect or missing items, not-as-described items, and authenticity issues. Approved returns use a Poshmark-provided label. It is not an extra percentage deducted from seller payouts.

What is Seel Worry-Free Returns on Poshmark?

An optional buyer-paid add-on being tested for eligible U.S. transactions (announced January 2026). It gives buyers extra return flexibility such as fit or change of mind, while seller earnings and payout timing are not affected and returned items go to Seel rather than the seller.

How do I price an item to keep a target amount on Poshmark?

For sales of $15 or more, divide your target by 0.80 (to keep $40, price at $50; to keep $60, price at $75). Under $15, add the $2.95 flat fee to your target instead.

Why is my Poshmark payout lower than 80% of my listing price?

The fee applies to the final order price after offers and discounts, not the listing price. Check the final transaction price, seller or bundle discounts, overweight label upgrades, and any other line items in the transaction record before assuming Poshmark took more than 20%.
