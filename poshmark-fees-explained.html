How Much Does Poshmark Take? The True Cost of Selling on Poshmark (2026)

I still remember the first time I sold a $60 pair of vintage Levi's on Poshmark. I did the math in my head: buy for $10, sell for $60, make $50 profit. Easy money. Then the item sold, the buyer paid, and I opened my Poshmark earnings tab. Poshmark had taken $12. I kept $48. Wait, what about the shipping? What about the payment processing?

If you've ever stared at your Poshmark balance and wondered, "How much does Poshmark actually take from my sale?" you aren't alone. The 20% commission sticker shock is the number one complaint in reseller communities. But here is the secret that veteran sellers know: Poshmark's fee structure is completely misunderstood. For a $12 t-shirt, it's a brutal trap. For a 4-pound winter coat, it's actually cheaper than eBay.

Today, we are opening the Seller's Ledger. We are going to dissect three real sales, break down the exact Poshmark fee structure for 2026 (including a brand-new Texas tax nobody is talking about and the Seel Worry-Free Returns change that landed this year), and show you exactly where your money goes.

The Golden Rule of Poshmark: For sales under $15, Poshmark takes a flat $2.95 commission. For sales $15 and over, Poshmark takes 20%, and you keep 80%. There are no listing fees, no monthly subscriptions, and the buyer pays for the standard shipping label.

Once I stopped listing under $15 solo items and stuck to the 2-5 lb sweet spot, my margins flipped positive.

The 5lb Shipping Trap (And How to Survive It)

That 5-pound limit is the most important number on Poshmark. The buyer pays $6.49 for shipping, and Poshmark provides a USPS Ground Advantage label that covers up to 5 lbs.

But what happens if a buyer bundles three pairs of jeans and the box hits 7 pounds? Poshmark doesn't just eat the extra cost. They deduct an overweight shipping upgrade fee directly from your earnings.

Posh Protect: The Free Insurance You Didn't Know You Had

Here's the part of Poshmark's fee structure that nobody talks about — because it's free and it's always there. Posh Protect comes with every single order, and it's the reason the 20% fee doesn't feel like a rip-off to long-time sellers.

Posh Protect covers three things:

  • Lost packages: If USPS loses your shipment, Poshmark refunds the buyer AND pays you. You don't fight with USPS claims. Poshmark eats the loss out of their own funds.
  • Damaged items: If the item arrives damaged in transit (not from buyer misuse), Posh Protect steps in.
  • Not-as-described claims: Buyers can open a case within 3 days of delivery for items that don't match the listing.

On eBay, if a package is lost, you file a claim with USPS and wait weeks for a response. On Poshmark, Poshmark handles it and pays you immediately. That alone is worth the 20% for many sellers.

Seel Worry-Free Returns: The 2025 Game-Changer

Here's the biggest Poshmark change most sellers have missed entirely. In 2025, Poshmark partnered with Seel to offer optional "Worry-Free Returns" that buyers can add at checkout. This is a massive shift in how Poshmark handles returns, and it affects your fees in ways you should know.

What Seel does: Buyers can pay a small premium at checkout to get 7-day return flexibility (instead of Poshmark's standard 3-day "not as described" window). If they use it, Seel handles the return and refund — not you.

What this means for your fees: Your earnings as a Poshmark seller stay exactly the same. Seel pays the refund out of their own funds. The item goes to Seel's warehouse, not back to you. You don't lose the 20% commission, you don't lose the item, and you don't deal with the return process. Seel returns are effectively free insurance for you.

What you can't control: Sellers cannot opt out of Seel. Buyers choose whether to add it at checkout. Bundle purchases are currently excluded.

The practical rule: If a buyer adds Seel protection and returns for fit or change-of-mind reasons, you keep your earnings and the item goes to Seel. If they open a "not as described" case through Poshmark directly (the traditional 3-day NAD claim), normal Poshmark return rules apply. The "Recurring Fee" Panic (A Bank Statement Myth)

Let's address the biggest panic in the Poshmark seller forums: "Why did Poshmark charge me $10? Does Poshmark charge recurring monthly fees?"

If you look at your credit card statement, you might see a charge from Poshmark labeled with the word "Recurring." Sellers freak out, thinking they accidentally signed up for a $10/month subscription.

Take a breath. You didn't. Poshmark does not charge recurring fees, listing fees, or monthly store subscriptions. The word "recurring" is simply a backend credit card processing code. It tells your bank, "Keep this card on file so the user doesn't have to type the numbers in every time they buy shipping supplies or upgrade a label." If you see a random $10 charge, it's because you bought closet covers, or you clicked "Upgrade Shipping Label" on a heavy bundle. It is not a subscription.

The 2026 Texas Seller Fee Tax (What Nobody Is Telling You)

If you live in Texas, your Poshmark ledger just got a new line item. As of October 1, 2025, the state of Texas imposed a Seller Fee Tax on marketplace commissions.

This is not a sales tax on the buyer. This is a tax on Poshmark's fee, which Texas law now requires Poshmark to pass on to you. Texas applies an ~8.25% tax to 80% of the seller fee.

"Wait, they are taxing my fee?" Yes. If you sell a $100 item in Texas, Poshmark takes their $20 fee. Texas taxes 80% of that fee ($16) at 8.25%, resulting in a $1.32 deduction from your earnings. It's small, but if you do high volume in Texas, it adds up. The tax shows up separately in your My Sales Report under "Texas Seller Fee Tax." (Always consult a tax professional regarding your specific state laws).

Why Are Poshmark Fees So High? (The Hidden Value)

People constantly ask, "Why are Poshmark fees so high compared to Mercari or eBay?" Because you aren't just paying for a marketplace. You are paying for three massive things:

  1. The $6.49 Flat Rate Shipping: Buyers love Poshmark because they know exactly what shipping will cost, whether they are buying a silk blouse or a 4lb pair of boots. This increases conversion rates.
  2. Posh Protect: Poshmark holds the funds in escrow. If a package is lost in transit, Poshmark refunds the buyer and pays you out of their own pocket. You don't have to fight with USPS claims.
  3. The Social Algorithm: Poshmark is a social network. The "Posh Parties," the sharing, the followers — that infrastructure costs money to run. You are paying a 20% marketing fee to have your items pushed to millions of active scrollers.

The 3-Day Return Guillotine

We cannot talk about Poshmark fees without talking about how you lose them. Poshmark has the strictest return policy in the resale game.

Unlike eBay, where buyers can sometimes return items for "remorse," all Poshmark sales are final unless the item is Not As Described (NAD). But here is the catch: The buyer only has 3 days from delivery to open an NAD case. If they wait 4 days, or if they click "Accept Order," the funds are instantly released to you, and the sale is locked forever.

However, if they do open a valid NAD case within 3 days, Poshmark will force the return. The commission reverses, the buyer gets their money back, and you get the item back — hopefully in the same condition you sent it. If it comes back damaged or swapped, you fight that battle next. We cover the full 72-hour return countdown and how to win appeals in what you lose on a Poshmark damaged return.

The one exception to the 3-day rule: buyers who added Seel Worry-Free Returns at checkout get 7 days, and Seel handles the return independently. Your earnings stay intact either way.

Can I Write Off Poshmark Fees on My Taxes?

Yes — and this is where a lot of sellers leave money on the table. The 20% commission and the $2.95 flat fee are both ordinary business expenses deductible on Schedule C. So are any overweight bundle fees you paid and the Texas Seller Fee Tax if you live in Texas.

The key is documentation. Poshmark provides a My Sales Report that shows every fee deducted. Export it at year-end and hand it to your tax professional. We explain the full tax write-off process for resellers in how to write off forced-return losses.

FAQ

How much fees does Poshmark take?
$2.95 flat for sales under $15; 20% commission for sales $15 and over. No listing fees, no monthly subscriptions.
How much does Poshmark take from a $100 sale?
Exactly $20. You keep $80, assuming the package weighs under 5 lbs.
Why are Poshmark fees so high?
The 20% bundles payment processing, Posh Protect insurance, and a subsidized flat-rate shipping label for the buyer.
Does Poshmark charge recurring fees?
No. "Recurring" on your bank statement is just a card-saving code, not a monthly subscription.
Is it cheaper to sell on Poshmark or eBay?
For items over 2 lbs, Poshmark is usually cheaper because the buyer pays a flat $6.49 shipping rate, whereas eBay sellers must buy expensive heavy-weight labels out of pocket.
Does Seel Worry-Free Returns affect my seller fees?
No. When a buyer adds Seel protection, Seel handles the return and refund out of their own funds. Your earnings stay intact.
What is Posh Protect?
Posh Protect is the free buyer/seller protection built into every Poshmark order. It covers lost packages, damaged items, and not-as-described claims within 3 days of delivery.
Can I write off Poshmark fees on my taxes?
Yes. The 20% commission, $2.95 flat fee, and any overweight bundle fees are ordinary business expenses deductible on Schedule C.
