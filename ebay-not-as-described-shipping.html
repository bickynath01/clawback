Who Pays Return Shipping on eBay "Not as Described"? (2026 Answer: Usually You)

On an eBay "not as described" (NAD) return, you pay the return shipping — and here's the part that catches every seller off guard: the label cost is often deducted from your refund before you even see it. You sell a $60 jacket, the buyer claims it doesn't match the listing, eBay generates a $9 return label, and when the refund processes you see $51 instead of $60. The label came out of your funds automatically. This is the hidden cost that turns a simple return into a real loss, and it happens on virtually every NAD claim regardless of your return policy settings.

The brutal truth: Your "No Returns Accepted" policy means nothing on NAD claims. eBay Money Back Guarantee overrides it completely. You can set your policy to "No Returns," "30-Day Returns," or "60-Day Returns" — on a not-as-described claim, you're paying the return shipping every single time. This is by design.

The Shipping Label Autopsy: What Actually Happens

Most sellers never see the process behind the scenes. Here's exactly what happens when a buyer opens an NAD claim, step by step:

THE LABEL GENERATION PROCESS

Step 1: Buyer opens return with reason "Doesn't match description or photos." eBay's system flags this as NAD and automatically assigns shipping responsibility to the seller.
Step 2: You have 3 business days to respond with a resolution (accept return, offer partial refund, or provide your own label). If you don't respond, the buyer can ask eBay to step in.
Step 3: eBay generates a USPS Ground Advantage return label using the buyer's zip code and your return address. The system calculates weight based on your original listing.
Step 4: The label cost is calculated and reserved against your funds. You don't see it as a separate charge — it's just deducted when the refund processes.
Step 5: Buyer ships the item back within the return window (typically 7 days after label is provided).
Step 6: You receive the item, and the refund processes: original sale price minus the label cost. You see a lower number than expected.

Example: $60 sale - $9 label = $51 in your funds. You also lost the $0.40 per-order fee. Total loss: $9.40 before any value drop.

This automatic deduction is why so many sellers are surprised by the math. They see "refund processed" and assume they got their money back minus the sale. They don't realize the label cost came out separately. The CLAWBACK calculator shows you exactly what you're losing so there are no surprises.

Can You Provide Your Own Label? (Yes, And It Can Save Money)

Here's something most sellers don't know: you can upload your own return label instead of using eBay's. If you have access to discounted shipping rates through services like Pirateship, ShipStation, or your own USPS business account, you can generate a cheaper label and upload it to the return case.

The catch: the buyer still needs to be able to return the item easily. You can't make them drive 50 miles to drop it off. But if you can generate a label that's $3-5 cheaper than eBay's, that's real savings — especially at scale. A seller doing 50 returns per month could save $150-250 monthly just by providing their own labels.

Confession: For the first year I sold on eBay, I let eBay generate every return label. Then I realized I was paying $8-12 per return when Pirateship could generate the same label for $5-8. I switched to uploading my own labels and saved about $200/month. The process takes an extra 2 minutes per return but the savings are real. Now it's automatic: NAD claim comes in, I generate a label on Pirateship, upload it to the case, done.

When You CAN Fight The Label Cost

Most sellers assume the label cost is gone forever. But there are specific scenarios where you can appeal and get it refunded:

Scenario 1: Item matches your listing exactly. If the buyer claimed "not as described" but you have clear photos showing the item matches your listing, you can appeal after the return completes. Document with side-by-side photos: your listing photo vs. the item when it came back. If eBay sees the item is unchanged and matches your description, they may reverse the label cost.

Scenario 2: Buyer returned a different item. If the serial number doesn't match, or the item is clearly different from what you sent, that's return fraud. Photograph everything and open a case immediately. eBay takes swap scams seriously and will often refund the label cost plus the sale price.

Scenario 3: Buyer used the item (wardrobing). If the item comes back worn, stained, or damaged in a way that wasn't there when you shipped it, document with photos and appeal. You may get a partial refund of the label cost or a full reversal.

The key to winning these appeals: itemized evidence with photos. Sellers who send angry paragraphs lose. Sellers who send clear photos with timestamps and descriptions win. We cover the appeal process in detail in eBay return policy: 12 hidden costs.

How NAD Claims Hurt Your Seller Metrics (The Hidden Cost)

The label cost is the obvious expense. The hidden cost is how NAD claims affect your seller performance standing. Here's the mechanism:

eBay tracks your Item Not As Described (INAD) return rate as a percentage of your total transactions. On the 20th of each month, eBay evaluates your metrics. If your INAD rate is classified as "Very High" compared to other sellers in your category, you're charged an additional 5% on final value fees for sales in those categories the following month. Four consecutive months of Very High bumps it to 6%.

The compound effect: Let's say you sell $10,000/month in a category that gets hit with the 5% penalty. That's $500 in additional fees the next month — just from having too many NAD claims. The label costs are bad, but the penalty fees can be catastrophic. This is why preventing NAD claims is more important than fighting them after the fact.

How To Prevent NAD Claims (The Real Fix)

Fighting label costs after the fact is playing defense. The real fix is preventing NAD claims from happening in the first place. Here's what actually works:

1. Photograph from every angle. Front, back, sides, close-ups of any flaws, tags, serial numbers. The more photos, the harder it is for a buyer to claim "not as described" when you have proof.

2. Measure everything. Don't just say "medium." Give chest, waist, length, sleeve measurements in inches. Buyers who know the exact fit are less likely to claim it doesn't match.

3. List flaws prominently. If there's a small stain, say it in the first line of the description and show it in a photo. Buyers who know about flaws before buying can't claim surprise later.

4. Respond to questions fast. Buyers who ask questions and get detailed answers are less likely to open NAD claims. They feel informed. Buyers who don't get answers often buy anyway and then claim "not as described" when it's not what they expected.

5. Use video for high-value items. For anything over $100, a short video showing the item from all angles is the ultimate proof. It's much harder to dispute than photos.

The return shipping you pay on NAD claims is an ordinary business expense and is deductible on Schedule C. Track every label cost with your return records — one line per return showing the shipping cost paid. At year-end, that's a real deduction.

Real Scenarios: Three NAD Returns, Three Different Outcomes

Scenario 1: The Honest Mistake ($40 sale)

Buyer claimed a vintage t-shirt "didn't match the color in photos." You check your listing — the shirt is clearly a faded red, and you said "faded red" in the description. The item comes back and matches your photos exactly. You appeal with side-by-side photos and eBay reverses the $7 label cost. Total loss: $0.40 per-order fee only.

Scenario 2: The Wardrober ($80 sale)

Buyer claimed a dress "had a stain not shown in photos." The dress comes back with a faint makeup mark on the collar that wasn't there when you shipped. You have pre-shipment photos showing the collar was clean. You appeal with the photos and eBay gives you a partial refund of the label cost ($5 of $9). Total loss: $4 label + $0.40 per-order = $4.40.

Scenario 3: The Legitimate Claim ($120 sale)

Buyer claimed a camera lens "had fungus not mentioned in listing." You check your listing — you didn't mention it because you didn't see it. The lens comes back and you inspect it with a flashlight. There's faint fungus you missed. The claim is legitimate. You absorb the $14 label cost and the $30 value drop. Total loss: $14 label + $0.40 per-order + $30 value = $44.40.

FAQ

Who pays return shipping on an eBay not-as-described claim?
Usually the seller. eBay generates a return label and the cost is typically deducted from the seller's funds or from the refund amount.
How much does an eBay return label cost?
Return label costs vary by weight and distance. A 1-pound item shipped 500 miles costs around $6-8 via USPS Ground Advantage. A 5-pound item shipped cross-country can cost $12-18.
Can I provide my own return label on eBay NAD?
Yes, you can upload your own label instead of using eBay's. This can save money if you have access to discounted shipping rates through services like Pirateship or ShipStation.
What if the buyer is wrong about not-as-described?
You can appeal after the return completes. Document the item's condition with photos before shipping and after return. If you can prove the item matches your listing, eBay may reverse the label cost.
Does free returns change who pays NAD shipping?
With free returns enabled, you pay return shipping on ALL eligible returns including NAD. Without free returns, you still pay on NAD claims because eBay Money Back Guarantee overrides your return policy settings.
How do I reduce NAD claims?
Accurate photos from every angle, detailed measurements, listing any flaws clearly, and responding to buyer questions before purchase. NAD claims often come from expectation mismatches, not actual problems.
Does NAD shipping cost hurt my seller metrics?
The cost itself doesn't hurt metrics, but the NAD claim does. If your Item Not As Described rate is evaluated as Very High on the 20th of the month, you're charged an additional 5% on final value fees the following month.
Who pays return shipping on changed-mind returns?
If you have free returns disabled, the buyer pays. If you have free returns enabled, you pay. But on NAD claims, you pay regardless of your free returns setting.
