Who Pays Return Shipping on eBay When an Item Is "Not as Described"?

Getting an eBay return that says "item not as described" can be frustrating, especially when you know the listing was accurate. And the first question is usually pretty simple:

"Wait — why am I paying for this return?"

Here's the important part: if the buyer says the item was damaged, faulty, the wrong item, or didn't match your listing description, eBay generally requires the seller to accept the return — even if the listing says "no returns." In these situations, the seller is also generally responsible for the return shipping.

But the shipping label is only part of the cost. A return can also affect your refund, fee credits, resale value, and the amount of money you ultimately recover. This guide breaks down what actually happens, what to document, what to do if you think the buyer is wrong, and how to work out your real dollar loss.

Quick answer: Who pays eBay return shipping for "not as described"?

If the buyer's reason is that the item doesn't match the listing description, arrived damaged, is faulty, is missing parts or accessories, or is the wrong item, the seller will generally need to accept the return and cover the return shipping cost. This can apply even when the seller's normal return policy says they don't accept returns.

The key distinction is why the buyer is returning the item.

"But my listing says no returns. Why do I have to accept it?"

This catches a lot of sellers off guard. Your return policy controls many change-of-mind situations, but it doesn't necessarily override eBay's buyer-protection process.

eBay says buyers can still submit a return request even when a seller doesn't accept returns. If the reason is that the item was damaged, faulty, didn't match the listing, or the wrong item was received, the seller generally needs to accept the return.

So these are two different situations:

Buyer: "I changed my mind." → Your return policy may determine whether you have to accept that return.
Buyer: "This item doesn't match the description." → That's a different type of return and can fall under eBay's Money Back Guarantee even when you don't normally accept returns.

That's why simply writing "NO RETURNS" in a listing isn't a complete shield against INAD claims.

What counts as "not as described" on eBay?

The phrase can cover several different problems. For example:

  • the listing said "excellent condition" but the item arrived damaged
  • the listing showed one model but a different model was shipped
  • an important defect wasn't disclosed
  • an accessory shown in the listing wasn't included
  • the item doesn't match the photos or description
  • the buyer received something materially different from what was advertised

The important thing for sellers is not just the label on the return. Look at the actual complaint. Open the return request and check exactly what the buyer says is wrong. eBay's seller workflow shows the buyer's stated reason, comments, and any photos they provided.

How much can an eBay "not as described" return actually cost?

A $100 sale doesn't necessarily mean a $100 loss. Your actual exposure can involve several different pieces:

1. Return shipping

For an INAD-type return, the seller is generally responsible for the return shipping.

2. The refund

Depending on how the return is resolved, you may refund the buyer for the purchase.

3. Selling-fee credits

Eligible fee credits can reduce the financial impact, but they don't necessarily work the same way for every refund situation.

4. Lost item value

If the item comes back in worse condition, the financial hit may be larger than the shipping cost alone.

5. Your time

This one doesn't appear neatly on an eBay statement, but sellers know it is real: messages, photographs, return tracking, documentation, appeals, and relisting the item all take time.

That's why looking only at the refund amount can give you the wrong picture.

Does eBay refund the selling fees on an INAD return?

Sometimes — and the details matter.

eBay's current fee-credit policy says that when a buyer returns an item because it arrived damaged, was defective, was the wrong item, or didn't match the description/photos, a qualifying full refund can receive a credit for the variable final value fee. However, eBay says the per-order fixed fee is not credited in those INAD-type refund situations.

For a partial refund, the calculation is different again: eBay says eligible fee credits are proportional to the amount refunded. Its current example shows the refund percentage being applied to the eligible variable fees, while the fixed per-order amount is not credited.

So don't assume:

"I refunded the buyer, therefore every eBay fee disappeared."

That's not necessarily what happens. If you're specifically trying to understand the fixed fee: See our eBay per-order-fee return guide → That article goes deeper into the fee-credit calculation without making this return-shipping article unnecessarily complicated.

What if the buyer's "not as described" claim isn't true?

This is probably the part sellers worry about most. Maybe the listing clearly showed the flaw, the measurements were included, the item was exactly as photographed, the buyer misunderstood the description, the buyer simply changed their mind, or something about the returned item doesn't match what you originally shipped.

Your first instinct might be:

"This buyer is lying."

But that's not the strongest way to approach the situation with eBay. Evidence is stronger than frustration. Instead of arguing about who is telling the truth, build a record that lets someone else compare the two sides.

What evidence should an eBay seller keep?

Before shipping

  • listing photos and condition description
  • measurements and model numbers
  • serial numbers where applicable
  • identifying marks and included accessories
  • visible flaws and important listing details

When packing

  • the actual item and its serial number
  • included parts and condition
  • packaging

During the return

  • buyer's return reason and messages
  • buyer-provided photographs
  • tracking and return-label information

When the item arrives back

  • condition and missing accessories
  • new damage and signs of use
  • different serial numbers or identifying marks
  • whether the returned item appears to be the same item you shipped

The goal isn't to create a mountain of paperwork. The goal is to preserve the few pieces of evidence that answer the central question:

"Is the returned item actually the same item, in the same condition, that I sent?"

What if the buyer returns the item damaged?

This is where eBay's seller protections can become important. eBay says that if an eligible seller accepts a return and the item comes back in a different condition, the seller may be able to deduct up to 50% of the buyer's refund to recover lost value caused by the buyer's use, alteration, or damage. Eligibility and other requirements apply.

There is an important catch:

Don't treat that deduction as a general way to recover every cost of the return. eBay says sellers may use the protection to recover lost value caused by the item's changed condition — not simply to recover return postage or ordinary market losses. And if eBay steps in to resolve the return, the seller may lose the ability to make that deduction.

So if the returned item genuinely comes back damaged or materially different, document it promptly.

What if the buyer sends back a different item?

This is one of those situations where your original documentation becomes extremely valuable. Suppose you sold a collectible with a serial number. You photographed the serial number before shipping. The returned item has a different number. That's much stronger evidence than simply writing:

"The buyer sent me the wrong item."

The same principle applies to electronics, shoes, handbags, collectibles, cameras, watches, parts — anything with identifying marks. If an item is difficult to identify visually, documenting the identifying details before shipment can make a major difference if a return later becomes disputed.

What should you do when an INAD return is opened?

Don't ignore it. eBay says sellers generally have 3 business days to respond to a return request and try to resolve the issue.

Step 1 — Read the buyer's exact reason

Don't respond based only on the notification headline. Read the explanation and look at the buyer's photos.

Step 2 — Compare it with your listing

Check description, photographs, condition, measurements, model/version, and included accessories.

Step 3 — Respond through eBay

Keep your communication factual. Instead of "You're lying. I know exactly what I sent." try:

"The listing photographs show the item's condition before shipment, including the mark on the lower-left corner. The buyer's return request says the mark was not disclosed."

The second version gives eBay something concrete to evaluate.

Step 4 — Preserve your evidence

Don't wait until the return has already become a dispute. Save what you have while the transaction is still fresh.

What if you and the buyer can't agree?

If you respond to the request but don't reach an agreement after the applicable response period, you or the buyer can ask eBay to step in. eBay's current seller guidance says sellers generally have 3 business days to respond before this escalation can occur.

If eBay steps in, the outcome can matter beyond the immediate refund. For example, eBay says that when it steps in, an eligible seller may no longer be able to use the deduction for a returned item that came back used or damaged. That's another reason to handle the return carefully rather than simply letting the deadline pass.

Can you appeal an eBay return decision?

Yes, in eligible situations. If eBay has stepped in and you disagree with the resolution, eBay currently says sellers can appeal by providing new information within 30 calendar days of the case being closed. The important word is new. An appeal isn't simply "I disagree with the decision."

Useful supporting information can include tracking information, proof that a returned item was lost or delivered incorrectly, documents showing that the item matched the listing, proof of a refund, and other relevant information that wasn't previously considered.

If you're going to appeal, make the evidence easy to understand. A short timeline can help:

Listed→Shipped→Buyer claim→Return→Returned condition→Evidence→Requested resolution

What if you don't respond to the return?

This is one situation where ignoring the notification can create a much bigger headache. eBay says that if a seller doesn't respond to an INAD-type return request, eBay may refund the buyer and seek reimbursement from the seller, in some cases without requiring the buyer to return the item.

Don't simply disappear from the return request. Respond through the eBay process and preserve your evidence.

Does an INAD return affect your eBay seller metrics?

It can. But don't assume that one return automatically means your account is suddenly in trouble. eBay has specific service-metric rules around its "Item not as described" rate and seller performance. There are also circumstances where a return may be excluded from that metric, including certain situations involving abusive buyer activity or seller protections.

The important takeaway is: pay attention to patterns, not just one isolated return. If you're seeing repeated INAD claims, look for patterns in condition descriptions, measurements, photographs, compatibility information, packaging, missing accessories, and recurring buyer misunderstandings.

Sometimes the best defense against the next INAD isn't fighting the current one. It's making the next listing harder to misunderstand.

How to reduce eBay "not as described" returns

  • Show the actual item. For used goods, don't rely entirely on stock images. Show the exact condition of the item you're shipping.
  • Photograph flaws. If there's a scratch, stain, crease, chip, repair, discoloration, or other imperfection, show it clearly.
  • Use measurements. Particularly useful for clothing, shoes, furniture and other items where buyers interpret size differently.
  • Identify the exact model. For electronics, parts and collectibles, model numbers and identifying marks prevent confusion.
  • Don't hide behind vague condition language. Explain what "good" actually means for that particular item.
  • Keep your listing consistent. Title, photos, description and item specifics should tell the same story.

The part sellers often miss: your "loss" isn't always the refund

Let's say you sold an item for $100. The buyer opens an INAD return. You refund the buyer. At first glance, it looks like: "I lost $100." But that's not necessarily your final economic loss.

You may recover some eligible eBay fees. The item may come back and still be worth $100 — or it may come back worth $70. You may have paid return shipping. You may have a fixed fee that isn't credited. You may be able to recover some lost value if an eligible returned item comes back damaged. Those pieces matter.

That's exactly why looking only at the buyer's refund can be misleading.

What I would save for every return

If you sell enough on eBay, having a simple return folder can save you a lot of headaches. For each significant return, keep:

  • Listing: screenshots, photos, description, item specifics
  • Transaction: order details, refund amount, fee information
  • Return: buyer's reason, messages, tracking, return information
  • Returned item: photos, condition, serial/identifying information, missing parts
  • Resolution: refund, fee credits, eBay decision, appeal information if applicable

You don't need to archive every $8 transaction forever. But for expensive, fragile, collectible, or high-risk items, this documentation can be worth the few minutes it takes.

eBay "Not as Described" Return FAQ

Does eBay make sellers pay return shipping for an item not as described?

Generally, yes. If the buyer's reason is that the item was damaged, faulty, didn't match the listing, or was the wrong item, eBay's current seller guidance says the seller is generally responsible for the return shipping.

What if my eBay listing says no returns?

A no-returns policy doesn't necessarily prevent an INAD return. eBay says buyers can still submit a return request, and covered INAD situations generally require the seller to accept the return.

Does eBay refund the final value fee when a buyer says an item is not as described?

Eligible INAD-related full refunds can receive a credit for the variable final value fee under eBay's fee-credit rules. However, the fixed per-order fee isn't credited in those situations.

Does eBay refund the per-order fee on an INAD return?

Not for the INAD-type full-refund situations covered by eBay's current fee-credit policy. The variable final value fee can be credited, but the per-order fixed amount is not credited.

What happens if the buyer returns the item damaged?

Eligible sellers may be able to deduct up to 50% of the buyer's refund to recover lost value caused by buyer use, alteration or damage. Specific eligibility and timing requirements apply.

Can I deduct the return shipping cost from the buyer's refund?

Don't confuse the seller's return-shipping responsibility with the separate protection for lost item value. eBay says the deduction protection is for loss in value caused by the item's changed condition; it isn't a general deduction for return postage or market losses.

What if the buyer falsely claims the item was not as described?

Document the listing, condition, photographs, messages and returned item, then use eBay's return/reporting process. eBay says sellers can report a buyer when they believe a false claim has been made, and certain eligible seller protections may apply if eBay determines the claim was false.

Can I appeal if eBay rules against me?

If eBay has stepped in and closed the case, sellers can appeal by providing new information within 30 calendar days of the case being closed.

How long do I have to respond to an eBay return request?

eBay currently says sellers generally have 3 business days to respond and work toward resolving the return.

What if the buyer sends back a different item?

Document the discrepancy immediately. Compare serial numbers, identifying marks, accessories, photographs and condition with the evidence you kept before shipping. Then provide that evidence through the appropriate eBay return process.

Can an INAD return affect my seller metrics?

It can, depending on the circumstances and your overall service metrics. However, don't assume that one return automatically creates a serious account problem. eBay has specific rules and exclusions for its "Item not as described" metric.

How can I reduce INAD returns?

Make the listing difficult to misunderstand: use accurate photos, disclose flaws, include measurements where useful, identify the exact model/version, list included accessories, and keep your description consistent with the photographs.

Bottom line

An eBay "item not as described" return isn't just a shipping problem. For a seller, the real question is:

After the refund, shipping, fee credits, returned-item condition and any protection are all accounted for — what did this transaction actually cost me?

That's the number worth knowing. And once you know that number, you can start looking for patterns: which returns are costing you the most, which problems keep repeating, which listings need better descriptions, and which types of items carry the biggest return risk. That's much more useful than simply knowing that a buyer opened a return.
