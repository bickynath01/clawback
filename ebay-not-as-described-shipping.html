Who Pays Return Shipping on eBay "Not as Described"?

In most not-as-described (NAD) cases, the seller ends up paying the return shipping. That's often the hidden cost that turns a refund into a real loss.

How NAD return shipping works

When a buyer claims an item isn't as described, eBay generally sides with the buyer and provides a return path. The return shipping label is commonly charged to the seller (sometimes deducted from the refund). So on top of refunding the sale and losing any per-order fee, you're out the cost of getting the item back.

Who ultimately pays can depend on the case details and your seller standing — but plan for it landing on you.

Worked example

A $60 sale, a not-as-described return, $8.50 of return shipping you paid, and the item comes back damaged — now worth $25 instead of $60. Your loss: the per-order fee eBay keeps, the $8.50 shipping, and the $35 value drop = $43.90. That's the number most sellers never calculate.

FAQ

Who pays NAD return shipping?
Usually the seller — the cost commonly falls on you via a return label.
Is it deducted from my refund?
Often yes; the label cost can come out of what's returned to you.
Can I avoid it?
Accurate listings and photos reduce NAD claims — but when one happens, the calculator shows your true exposure.
