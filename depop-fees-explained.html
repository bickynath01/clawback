How Much Does Depop Take From a Sale? Depop Fees Explained (2026)

You sell a $50 item on Depop, open the receipt, and wonder: "How much did Depop actually take?"

If you're still subtracting the old 10% selling fee from every sale, your math may be out of date. For sellers in the US, UK, and Australia, Depop currently charges no seller selling fee. But that does not mean every sale is completely free. Payment processing still applies, and a qualifying Boosted Listing sale can add another fee.

And if you're outside those three markets, the old 10% selling fee can still apply under Depop's current rules.

So the real question isn't simply: "Does Depop charge a fee?" It's: "Which fees apply to my sale, what are they calculated on, and what will I actually keep?" That's what this guide breaks down.

The important 2026 change: the old 10% rule is not universal anymore

This is where a lot of older Depop advice goes wrong. If you're selling from the US, UK, or Australia, Depop currently says there is no seller selling fee. Australia is especially important because its seller-fee structure has changed under Depop's 2026 rules.

So an older article saying:

"Depop takes 10% from every sale"

isn't a safe 2026 answer.

For sellers outside those three markets, however, Depop still lists a 10% selling fee where its current rules apply. Your country matters. Your shipping method can matter. And whether the sale was boosted can matter.

What fees can actually come out of a Depop sale?

There are three seller-side fee categories you should know.

1. Selling fee

This is the old 10% fee that many sellers still remember. For sellers in the US, UK, and Australia: 0%. For sellers outside those markets, Depop currently lists a 10% selling fee under the applicable rules.

2. Payment-processing fee

This is the standard transaction fee for Depop Payments in the US, UK, and Australia.

  • US: 3.3% + $0.45
  • UK: 2.9% + £0.30
  • Australia: 2.6% + A$0.30

The processing percentage applies to the item price + shipping + applicable taxes. That last part is easy to miss.

3. Boosting fee

Boosting is optional, but if a sale qualifies as a Boosting sale, the fee can be significant. For new listings under the current Boosted Listings policy:

  • US: 12%
  • UK: 12%
  • Australia: 8%
  • Outside UK/US: 8%

The boosting fee is calculated on the item sale price excluding taxes and, when you arrange your own shipping, the shipping cost. Depop Shipping label costs are not included in the Boosting fee base.

How much does Depop take from a $50 sale?

Let's make the math concrete. Assume: US seller, $50 item price, no shipping charged separately, no applicable tax in the example, no Boosting fee.

The processing fee is: $50 × 3.3% + $0.45 = $2.10

So: $50.00 − $2.10 = $47.90

You keep $47.90 before your other business costs. There is no 10% Depop selling fee in this US example.

That is why the old calculation ($50 − $5 = $45) is no longer the right US seller calculation.

The flat $0.45 matters more on cheap sales

The percentage gets most of the attention, but the fixed processing fee matters a lot when your item price is low. For a US seller with no shipping or tax in the example:

Notice what happens at the lower end. The fixed $0.45 doesn't change, so it represents a much larger percentage of a $10 sale than a $500 sale. That matters when you're deciding whether a low-priced item is worth listing.

What happens when the buyer pays shipping?

This is where the simple $50 example can become misleading. Depop says payment processing is calculated on: item price + shipping + applicable taxes.

So imagine: Item: $50, Buyer-paid shipping: $8, Tax: $0. Processing base: $50 + $8 = $58. US processing: $58 × 3.3% + $0.45 = $2.36.

The seller receives the $8 shipping amount as part of the transaction, but that does not mean the $8 is profit. You still have to account for the actual shipping label. So there are two separate numbers: Depop processing cost and actual shipping cost. Don't combine them.

Shipping is not a Depop fee — but it can be the bigger cost

This distinction is important when you're calculating real profit. Suppose: Item sale: $50, Buyer pays $8 shipping, Processing: $2.36, Your actual label: $7.48.

Your transaction looks like: $58.00 received before fees minus $2.36 processing minus $7.48 shipping label = $48.16.

That's very different from saying: "Depop only took $2.36, so I made $55.64." You didn't. The shipping label is not a Depop fee, but it is still a real seller expense. That's why CLAWBACK separates platform fees from actual business costs.

What does Depop take if I boost my listing?

This is the fee that can dramatically change the calculation. Under Depop's current Boosted Listings policy, a qualifying boosted sale in the US or UK carries a 12% boosting fee. Australia uses 8%.

But there is an important detail:

You don't pay the Boosting fee merely because your listing is displayed as boosted.

Depop says a Boosting fee can apply when a buyer interacts with your boosted listing — including viewing, clicking, or liking it — and then purchases the item within 28 days. The buyer doesn't have to click the boosted tile; simply seeing the boosted version can qualify the sale. And if you unboost after that view, the fee can still apply if the buyer purchases within the applicable 28-day window.

Example: a $50 US boosted sale

Suppose: Item price: $50, No shipping, No applicable tax, US seller, Sale qualifies for Boosting.

  • Payment processing: $50 × 3.3% + $0.45 = $2.10
  • Boosting: $50 × 12% = $6.00
  • Total seller fees: $2.10 + $6.00 = $8.10
  • Amount remaining before other business costs: $50 − $8.10 = $41.90

That's a very different result from the non-boosted example ($47.90). So the 0% selling-fee headline does not tell the whole story.

Depop Shipping vs. your own shipping changes the Boosting calculation

This is an easy detail to miss. Depop says the Boosting fee applies to: item sale price excluding taxes + shipping costs when you arrange your own shipping.

So if you use your own shipping method, the shipping amount can become part of the Boosting fee base. If you use a Depop Shipping label, the label cost is not part of the Boosting fee base.

Payment processing is different: Depop's seller-fee policy says processing is based on the item price plus shipping and applicable taxes. So don't assume the different fee categories all use the same calculation base. They don't.

What is the Depop Marketplace fee?

There is another Depop fee that sellers often see mentioned: the Marketplace fee. But this is important:

The Marketplace fee is paid by the buyer.

For buyers in the US and UK buying in USD or GBP, Depop says the Marketplace fee can be: up to 5% of the item purchase price + up to $1/£1 excluding taxes and shipping. Australia has a similar buyer-side Marketplace fee of up to 5% + A$1 under its current policy.

You should not subtract the buyer's Marketplace fee from the seller's payout when calculating what you keep. It is a separate buyer charge.

Does the buyer Marketplace fee affect sellers at all?

Not directly as a seller fee. But it can still matter commercially because the buyer sees a higher checkout total. The Marketplace fee doesn't come out of your seller proceeds, but the buyer's final price can affect how attractive your listing feels compared with another platform.

So: not your fee ≠ irrelevant to your sale. It's simply a different part of the transaction.

What about Depop bundles?

Bundles have a special fee rule. For sellers outside the UK and US, Depop says the selling fee applies per item in the bundle. The payment-processing fee applies once per bundle transaction.

So if a seller outside the UK and US sells three items in one bundle, they can see: 3 × selling-fee charges but 1 × payment-processing charge. That's why bundle receipts can look strange if you're expecting one percentage applied to the entire order.

For US, UK, and Australian sellers, there is no seller selling fee under the current rules, so this particular 10%-per-item issue doesn't apply.

How are Depop fees actually deducted?

The timing depends on how you were paid.

Depop Payments

Depop says applicable fees are deducted before your sale proceeds are paid to your bank account. So you generally see the transaction after the applicable fees have already been accounted for.

PayPal

For sellers using PayPal, Depop says the sale proceeds go to your PayPal account and the Depop selling fee and PayPal processing fee are deducted separately. Depop says PayPal-related fees can sometimes be charged later, including up to 21 days after a sale.

How do I check exactly what Depop charged me?

Don't guess from the payout amount. Open the actual receipt. For Depop Payments, Depop's current instructions say you can review your recent activity through:

My Depop → My Account → Depop Balance → Latest Transactions

Depop also provides a sales download that can include sale date, buyer, item, pricing, applicable fees, and shipping information.

7 Depop fee mistakes sellers still make

  1. "Depop takes 10% from every sale." Not anymore for US, UK, and Australian sellers. The current seller-fee policy says those markets have no Depop selling fee.
  2. "No selling fee means Depop is completely free." Not quite. Payment processing still applies, and qualifying Boosted sales can add a Boosting fee.
  3. "Processing is calculated only on my item price." No. Depop says payment processing includes: item price + shipping + applicable taxes.
  4. "Shipping money is profit." No. Buyer-paid shipping is part of the transaction, but you still have to pay the actual shipping cost.
  5. "The Marketplace fee comes out of my payout." No. The Marketplace fee is a buyer-side charge. Don't subtract it from your seller proceeds.
  6. "If I turn Boosting off, I can't be charged later." Not necessarily. If a buyer saw the boosted listing and purchases within the applicable 28-day period, Depop says the Boosting fee can still apply.
  7. "The fee percentage tells me my real profit." It doesn't. Your actual profit can also depend on item cost, shipping, packaging, returns, refunds, discounts, Boosting, and other business expenses.

Your Depop fee is not your true selling cost

This is where the CLAWBACK approach is different. A fee calculator can tell you: Depop took $X. But a seller usually needs to know: "How much money did I actually make?" Those aren't the same calculation.

Platform cost: selling fee + processing + BoostingvsBusiness cost: inventory + label + packaging + returns + refunds

Your real result is what remains after both.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Depop take from a sale in 2026?

For sellers in the US, UK, and Australia, Depop currently charges no seller selling fee. Payment processing still applies: 3.3% + $0.45 in the US, 2.9% + £0.30 in the UK, and 2.6% + A$0.30 in Australia. Sellers outside those markets can still have a 10% selling fee under Depop's applicable rules.

Does Depop still charge a 10% selling fee?

Not for sellers based in the US, UK, or Australia. Depop currently lists a 10% selling fee for sellers outside those markets where its selling-fee rules apply.

What is the Depop seller fee in the US?

The US seller selling fee is currently 0%. US sellers using Depop Payments still pay 3.3% + $0.45 in payment processing on the item price plus shipping and applicable taxes.

What is the Depop seller fee in the UK?

The UK seller selling fee is currently 0%. UK Depop Payments processing is 2.9% + £0.30 on the item price plus shipping and applicable taxes.

What is the Depop seller fee in Australia?

Australia currently has a 0% Depop selling fee. Payment processing is 2.6% + A$0.30.

Does Depop charge a percentage of shipping?

Payment processing is calculated on the item sale price plus shipping costs and applicable taxes. For sellers outside the US, UK, and Australia, the 10% selling fee can also include shipping when the seller arranges their own shipping.

How much is Depop Boosting?

For qualifying Boosted sales, the current rate is 12% in the US and UK and 8% in Australia and markets outside the UK and US under Depop's current policy.

Do I pay a Boosting fee just for turning Boosting on?

No. Depop says a Boosting fee can apply when a buyer interacts with your boosted listing — including viewing, clicking, or liking it — and then purchases the item within 28 days.

Does Depop charge a monthly fee?

Depop's current seller-fee policy does not list a standard monthly subscription fee.

Does Depop charge a listing fee?

Depop's current seller-fee policy does not list a standard seller listing fee.

What is the Depop Marketplace fee?

The Marketplace fee is a buyer-side fee. In the US and UK, it can be up to 5% of the item purchase price plus up to $1/£1, excluding taxes and shipping. Australia has a similar buyer-side fee of up to 5% plus A$1.

Does the Marketplace fee come out of my seller payout?

No. It is charged to the buyer separately and should not be subtracted from your seller proceeds.

Why was I charged a Depop fee if the selling fee is 0%?

Because 0% selling fee does not mean 0% transaction costs. Payment processing and qualifying Boosting fees can still apply.

How are Depop fees deducted?

With Depop Payments, applicable fees are deducted before sale proceeds are paid to your bank. With PayPal, Depop says the sale proceeds go to PayPal and the selling and PayPal processing fees are deducted separately.

Can Depop fees be refunded?

Yes. When a sale is refunded, Depop says the applicable selling, payment-processing, and Boosting fees are reversed. Depop Payments handles this automatically; PayPal sellers outside the US, UK, and Australia may need to contact Support for the Depop selling-fee refund.

How does Depop charge fees on bundle sales?

For sellers outside the UK and US, Depop says the selling fee applies per item in a bundle, while payment processing applies once per bundle transaction.

Where can I see exactly what Depop charged me?

Open your transaction receipt in Depop. Depop also provides a sales download containing transaction information including applicable fees and shipping details.

The bottom line

The old "Depop takes 10%" answer is no longer good enough for 2026. For sellers in the US, UK, and Australia, the current seller selling fee is 0%.

But your actual seller cost can still include: payment processing + Boosting + shipping + your other business expenses. And those costs don't all use the same calculation base.

So don't price from a remembered percentage. Check the current rules, check the transaction receipt, and run the actual numbers for the sale you're making.
