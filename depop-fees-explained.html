How Much Does Depop Take From a Sale? Depop Fees Explained (2026)

A jacket sells for $50 and, like every Depop seller does, the balance gets checked before the buyer's even confirmed receipt — just to see what's left. $45 after the 10%. Then processing hits. Then the label that already got bought. By the time the dust settles, a "$50" sale turns into about $36 in the bank. That gap between the listing price and what actually lands in the account is the entire subject of this guide.

Depop's fees aren't hidden exactly, but they're scattered across help pages, and a lot of the advice floating around is outdated. Below is the current rulebook, run against real sales, plus the myths that quietly make sellers underprice their own stuff. Anyone who's typed "how much does depop take from a sale" into Google at some point — this is the plain-English answer, with a calculator to get an exact number instead of an estimate.

The short answer: Depop takes a 10% selling fee on the total (item + shipping) plus a payment processing fee (roughly 2.9% + $0.30, varying by region). There's no monthly fee and no listing fee. On a $50 sale, expect around $6.75 in fees before shipping.

That's the general math — but the real number depends on the actual sale price and how much shipping's being charged. Plug in the real numbers below to see the real payout, not a rounded estimate.

Myth vs. reality: 8 things sellers get wrong

MYTH #1

"Depop takes 10% — that's the only fee."

REALITY

The 10% is the loud one, but the payment processing fee is the quiet second bite. On a $50 sale that's another ~$1.75. Sellers who budget only for the 10% are always surprised at payout — always add both.

MYTH #2

"The 10% is only on the item price."

REALITY

No — it's on the total transaction, including the shipping charged. Charge $8 shipping and Depop takes 10% of that too. This is why "free shipping" isn't actually free — the fee just gets baked into a bigger total either way.

MYTH #3

"Depop charges a monthly fee."

REALITY

It doesn't. No subscription, no listing fees for standard selling — payment only happens when something sells. That's genuinely seller-friendly compared to platforms that charge store subscriptions regardless of sales.

MYTH #4

"Shipping is profit."

REALITY

Shipping charged still gets taxed (see Myth #2), and the label itself is a real cost. Charge $8 shipping but pay $7.50 for the label, and the "shipping profit" is fifty cents — before the 10% on that $8. Price shipping honestly, not optimistically.

MYTH #5

"You get paid instantly."

REALITY

The balance updates when a sale completes, but the money isn't spendable until it lands in the bank — typically 1–3 business days later. Plan cash flow around that lag, especially when reinvesting straight into new inventory.

MYTH #6

"Boosts are worth it for everyone."

REALITY

Boosts only pay off if the margin survives the extra cost. On a $15 tee with $6 of fees already, a boost can eat the entire profit. On a $120 jacket, it's often genuinely worth it. Run the margin first, boost second.

MYTH #7

"Refunds are free."

REALITY

They're not. On Depop Payments the fees reverse when you refund — but on PayPal sales the processing fee stays, and return shipping often lands on the seller. The full breakdown is here — are Depop fees refunded when you refund a buyer?

MYTH #8

"Depop fees are the same everywhere."

REALITY

The selling fee percentage and processing fees vary by country. A UK seller and a US seller aren't paying identical numbers. Check the region on the official help page before pricing anything.

How much does Depop take on a $50 sale? (Quick math)

$5 selling fee + ~$1.75 processing = ~$6.75, before the label. So a "$50" sale is really a $43.25 sale before shipping costs. If the item itself cost $20 and the label $7, true profit lands around $16. Worth knowing that number before listing, not after the sale's already done.

Is Depop worth it for sellers?

Honestly — for clothing and streetwear, yes. The audience is genuinely there, and there's no monthly fee quietly draining money while items sit unsold. For heavy or cheap items, the flat label cost plus the percentage fee can squeeze margins hard. Depop rewards higher-priced, easily shippable fashion; anyone selling $10 trinkets is fighting the math from the start. (Compare payouts across all four platforms here.)

How to keep more of every sale

  1. Price in both fees, not just the 10%. Add roughly 2.9% + $0.30 for processing on top.
  2. Weigh items and price shipping from real label costs, not guesses.
  3. Skip boosts on low-margin items; save them for $80+ pieces where the math actually works.
  4. Track every fee so tax season turns processing and shipping into real deductions. (Write-off guide.)
  5. Photograph flaws upfront to cut not-as-described refunds, which are the real margin killer. (Are Depop fees refunded?)

Conclusion: the gap is the business

The difference between the listing price and what actually lands in the bank is where reselling gets won or lost. Depop's two bites are fair when priced for and brutal when ignored. Learn the menu, run the math on every listing, and the "$50 sale" stops being a mystery — it becomes a number that was known in advance.
