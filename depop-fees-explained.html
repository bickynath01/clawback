How Much Does Depop Take From a Sale? Depop Fees Explained (2026)

You sell a $50 jacket, open the receipt and immediately wonder: "How much did Depop take?" If you're still mentally subtracting the old 10% selling fee, your math may be out of date. Depop's current fee structure is different depending on where you live, and the fee that actually matters may be payment processing, boosting, or both.

For sellers in the UK, US and Australia, Depop currently charges no Depop selling fee. But that does not mean selling is completely fee-free: payment processing still applies, and boosted sales can add a separate boosting fee. Sellers outside those markets can still face the 10% selling fee under Depop's current rules. The exact amount you keep therefore depends on your country, shipping setup, taxes and whether the sale qualifies for a boosted-listing fee.

The short answer (2026): Depop charges no selling fee to sellers based in the UK, US or Australia. Payment processing still applies: 2.9% + £0.30 in the UK, 3.3% + $0.45 in the US, and 2.6% + $0.30 AUD in Australia. Processing is charged on the item sale price plus shipping costs and applicable taxes. Boosted sales can add a separate fee.

That's the general math — but the real number depends on your sale price, what shipping you charge, and whether the item was boosted. Plug in the real numbers below to see the real payout, not a rounded estimate.

Myth vs. reality: 8 things sellers still get wrong

MYTH #1

"Depop takes 10% of every sale."

REALITY

Not for sellers based in the UK, US or Australia. Depop currently charges no selling fee in those three markets. Sellers outside those markets can still face the 10% selling fee under Depop's current rules. So if you're searching "Depop seller fee 2026" or "Depop 0 selling fees", the answer depends on where your seller account is based.

MYTH #2

"The processing fee is only on the item price."

REALITY

No. Depop says payment processing is charged on the item sale price plus shipping costs and applicable taxes. That's why a seller can see a processing fee that looks larger than expected even when there is no selling fee.

MYTH #3

"Depop charges a monthly fee or a standard listing fee."

REALITY

Depop's current seller-fee page does not list a monthly subscription or standard listing fee. The transaction fees that apply are tied to sales. If you're searching "Depop free fees" or "Depop 0 fees", don't confuse "no selling fee" with "no transaction costs at all" — processing and qualifying boosting fees can still apply.

MYTH #4

"Shipping is free money."

REALITY

Shipping isn't a platform fee, but the amount you charge for shipping can affect your processing fee, and you still have to pay the actual label cost when you arrange shipping. For example, if a buyer pays $8 for shipping, that $8 is part of the payment-processing base in the US. What you actually keep depends on the label cost too.

MYTH #5

"There is one universal Depop fee everywhere."

REALITY

There isn't. UK, US and Australia have different processing rates, while sellers outside those markets can still have a 10% selling fee plus PayPal processing. If you're comparing a US seller's receipt with a UK or Australian seller's receipt, the percentages may legitimately be different.

MYTH #6

"The 12% boosting fee is charged just for having a boost on."

REALITY

Depop's Boosted Listings policy says the fee becomes payable when a boosted listing qualifies as a boosting sale. In the UK and US the rate is 12%; Australia uses 8%; and Depop's current policy lists 8% outside the UK, US and Australia. Depop also says a buyer can purchase within 28 days of viewing a boosted listing for the sale to count as a boosting sale.

MYTH #7

"A refund automatically means I permanently lose the fees."

REALITY

Depop says that when a sale is refunded, the selling fee, payment processing fees and boosting fees are reversed. That is a fee rule, not a statement about your separate return-shipping or item-loss costs. For the detailed return/refund analysis, see our Depop refund-fee guide.

MYTH #8

"The 10% fee applies to the same amount as the processing fee."

REALITY

They have different fee bases. Payment processing is calculated on the item sale price plus shipping and applicable taxes. For sellers outside the UK, US and Australia, Depop says the 10% selling fee is based on the item sale price excluding taxes and can include shipping when the seller arranges their own shipping instead of using a Depop Shipping label. That difference matters when you're trying to reproduce a receipt exactly.

How Much Does Depop Take on a $50 Sale? (Quick Math)

If a US buyer pays $50 total and there is no separate shipping charge or applicable tax in the example, the payment-processing fee is:

$50 × 3.3% + $0.45 = $2.10.

So the seller receives $47.90 before any separate shipping-label cost or other business expenses. There is no 10% Depop selling fee for a US seller in this example.

If the buyer also pays shipping or applicable tax, the processing fee changes because Depop applies payment processing to the item price plus shipping and applicable taxes.

Why Does Depop Say You Pay a Fee When the Selling Fee Is $0?

This is where many sellers get confused. "No selling fee" does not mean "no fees." Depop can still charge payment processing, and a qualifying Boosted Listing sale can add a boosting fee.

So if your receipt says you were charged a fee, open the transaction receipt and look at the line items. Depop says sellers can review the fee breakdown from their sold-item receipts. That is the fastest way to distinguish a processing fee, selling fee, boosting fee or another transaction charge.

Is Depop Worth It for Sellers in 2026?

For a US, UK or Australian seller, the removal of the selling fee can materially change the margin calculation. But "worth it" still depends on your item price, shipping cost, processing fee and whether you pay for boosting. For low-priced items, a fixed processing fee and shipping cost can take a much larger share of the sale than they do on a higher-priced item.

How to keep more of every sale

  1. Price in the processing fee — use the rate for your market instead of automatically adding the old 10% selling fee.
  2. Separate fees from shipping costs — the label is not a Depop fee, but it can have a bigger effect on your real margin than the platform fee.
  3. Check the boost math before activating it — 12% in the UK/US or 8% in Australia and other applicable markets can materially change your margin.
  4. Track every cost so tax season turns processing and shipping into real deductions. (Write-off guide.)
  5. Photograph flaws upfront to cut not-as-described refunds — the fees reverse, but the label and value drop don't. (Are Depop fees refunded?)

Conclusion: the fee changed — the habit shouldn't

The 10% era is over for US sellers, and sellers still pricing around it are either overcharging (and losing sales) or flinching at margins that are actually fine now. The new rulebook is simple: one small proportional bite, one flat label tax, and optional boosting that demands a margin check. Learn the real menu, run the math on every listing, and the "$50 sale" stops being a mystery — it becomes a number that was known in advance.
