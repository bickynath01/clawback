Poshmark Damaged Returns: What Sellers Lose, What to Do When an Item Comes Back Worse (2026)

A Poshmark return is one problem. A Poshmark return that arrives back damaged, altered, incomplete, or different from what you shipped is a completely different problem.

The buyer may have already received a refund. The original sale may be gone. And now you are holding an item that may be worth less than it was when you sent it. That is the part most Poshmark return guides don't explain.

This guide is specifically for sellers dealing with a damaged or suspicious return: what Poshmark's rules actually say, what to document before shipping, what to do when the package comes back, and how to calculate the financial loss if the item's resale value has changed.

LAST CHECKED: SEPTEMBER 2026

The short answer

Under Poshmark's current Posh Protect policy, a buyer has 72 hours from delivery, based on the tracking information on the shipping label, to open a return case for issues such as undisclosed damage, an incorrect or missing item, an item not as described, or an authenticity issue. The buyer must provide supporting photos. If Poshmark approves the return, Poshmark provides the return label, and the buyer must ship the return within five days of approval.

The difficult seller scenario comes after that:

You receive the returned package and the item is now worse than when you shipped it.

Maybe there is a new stain. Maybe a tag is missing. Maybe the item has a cut or tear. Maybe parts of a bundle are missing. Maybe you suspect the returned item is not the same item you originally shipped.

At that point, don't start with an angry message.

Start with evidence.

What counts as a Poshmark return problem?

Poshmark's current return policy covers specific problems rather than ordinary buyer's remorse. The listed grounds include:

  • undisclosed damage
  • incorrect or missing item
  • item not as described
  • item not authentic

Buyers generally cannot use Posh Protect simply because an item does not fit or they changed their mind. That distinction matters to sellers because a return request does not automatically mean the buyer is entitled to return the item for any reason.

But once a return is approved, the seller's next concern becomes different:

What condition will the item be in when it comes back?

The seller problem nobody explains: the return can create a second loss

Imagine this: you sell a dress for $80. You photograph it before shipping. It has no stains, no holes and no visible damage. The buyer opens a Poshmark case. The return is approved. The item comes back with a new stain and you estimate that it could now sell for only $35.

The important number is not simply the original $80 sale price. The seller's economic problem is the change in the item's resale value.

Before shipmentAfter return
Estimated resale value: $80Estimated resale value after damage: $35
Value reduction: $45

That $45 does not appear as a neat line in your Poshmark account saying "damaged-return loss." You have to identify it yourself. That is exactly where a return-loss calculator is useful.

First: don't confuse a normal return with a damaged return

Normal approved return

The item comes back in essentially the condition in which it was sent. Your main issue is the reversed transaction and the time spent handling it.

Damaged return

The item comes back with a new condition problem. Now you have an additional economic question:

What is this item worth now compared with what it was worth before the return?

Suspicious return

The returned item appears to be: a different size, a different color, a different model, missing accessories, missing tags, missing pieces from a bundle, materially more worn, or newly damaged. This is not something you should simply treat as a normal return.

Document it immediately.

What to do before you ship the original order

The best damaged-return defense happens before the buyer ever receives the package. You want a record of what you actually shipped.

1. Photograph the item immediately before packing

  • Front, back, sides
  • Close-ups of important areas
  • Labels, size tags, brand tags
  • Serial numbers where applicable
  • Hardware, soles, zippers, closures
  • Accessories
  • Existing flaws

For valuable or easily substituted items, photograph identifying details that make the item distinguishable from another example of the same product. Don't rely entirely on your listing photos. Take a fresh set immediately before shipment.

2. Photograph the item and the shipping package together

You want a record connecting the exact item → the packaging → the shipment. For example: item front, item back, identifying detail, item inside packaging, sealed package, shipping label. The goal isn't a dramatic "proof video" — it's a simple evidence trail.

3. Keep your original listing intact

Your original listing can contain useful evidence: original photographs, condition description, measurements, disclosed flaws, color, size, included accessories. Poshmark's Seller Policy says that images and descriptions provided in listings and other parts of the platform may be reviewed and considered by Poshmark staff in a dispute. So don't casually delete or replace the original listing photos after a sale.

What recent sellers are actually worried about

Recent Poshmark seller discussions show recurring concerns around new damage appearing after a return, suspected item substitutions, and what evidence sellers can use when they believe the returned condition differs from what they shipped.

In March 2026, a seller reported receiving a sweater back with large new holes after a return. Other commenters recommended contacting Poshmark with before-and-after photographs. In January 2026, another seller reported a suspected jacket substitution and said they had photographs taken immediately before packaging. In March 2026, another seller reported receiving a damaged item back and described escalating the issue after Poshmark approved the buyer's return.

These are community reports, not proof that Poshmark routinely sides with buyers or sellers. But they reveal a recurring seller concern:

"How do I prove what I actually shipped if the item comes back different?"

The moment your return arrives: stop and document

If you receive a suspicious return, don't immediately clean it, repair it, wash it, remove anything, or throw away the packaging. First document the condition.

Photograph the package

  • The outside of the package
  • The shipping label
  • Visible damage to the package
  • Any unusual opening or resealing
  • The package before you open it

Then document the opening

For a high-value or particularly suspicious return, recording the opening can create a chronological record of what you received: package → label → opening → contents → condition. This is especially useful when the issue is not simply "the item is damaged" but "this isn't the item I shipped."

What if the buyer returned a different item?

Suppose you shipped a black leather jacket, size M, with a specific zipper pull and stitching detail — and the returned package contains a similar black jacket but different size, different zipper hardware or different condition. Don't turn the response into an argument about the buyer. Build an evidence package.

Then report the discrepancy through Poshmark's support process. A January 2026 seller discussion describes this exact concern: the seller said they had taken timestamped photos immediately before packaging, then the buyer alleged both damage and that the seller had sent a different jacket. That is anecdotal, but it illustrates why identifying details can matter more than simply photographing the item once.

What if the buyer damaged the item?

Examples include: new stain, new hole, cut fabric, marker or writing, broken hardware, missing tag, missing accessory, excessive wear, altered condition. Your first question should be:

Can I demonstrate that this condition was different before shipment?

That's why before-and-after evidence matters: before shipment, a clear photograph of the area, the listing photograph, the condition description, identifying details and date/time information where available; after return, the same area photographed again, a close-up of new damage, complete item photographs and packaging photographs. The closer the comparison, the easier it is for a reviewer to understand the actual dispute.

What if the damage happened during shipping?

Don't automatically assume the buyer caused it. There are several possibilities: damage existed before shipment, buyer caused the damage, return shipping caused the damage, packaging was inadequate, or the evidence is inconclusive.

Poshmark's current policy says sellers are responsible for proper packaging and states that Poshmark is not liable for damage caused by improper or inadequate seller packaging. So if the package itself arrives damaged, photograph the packaging before opening it. That creates evidence for a different type of dispute.

What if the buyer says the item was already damaged?

Don't respond with "You're lying." Instead answer the actual claim. For example:

"The listing photographs show this area without the damage described in the claim. I have also attached photographs taken immediately before shipment showing the same condition."

That is stronger because it gives the reviewer something concrete to compare. Poshmark's Seller Policy specifically states that images and descriptions can be reviewed and considered by Poshmark staff in a dispute.

Your 72-hour clock is the buyer's clock — not your evidence window

The buyer's current Posh Protect deadline is 72 hours after delivery, measured using the tracking information on the shipping label. That doesn't mean you should wait three days to prepare. If a return case appears, start collecting your evidence immediately. And if a returned item arrives damaged, don't assume the original case automatically tells the whole story.

Treat the condition of the returned item as its own evidence problem.

What happens after a return is approved?

Poshmark's current policy says that, when a return is approved, Poshmark provides the buyer with a return label. The buyer must ship the return within five days of approval to remain eligible for a refund. The seller's practical checklist is therefore:

Before the return arrives

  • Save the case information
  • Save your original listing photos
  • Save your pre-shipment photographs
  • Note the exact problem claimed by the buyer
  • Don't delete relevant messages

When the return arrives

  • Photograph the package
  • Photograph the shipping label
  • Photograph the packaging condition
  • Document the opening if appropriate
  • Photograph the entire returned item
  • Photograph every new problem
  • Compare the item with your original evidence
  • Contact Poshmark through the appropriate support channel

Don't make this mistake: cleaning the item first

If a returned garment has a stain, your first instinct may be to wash it. Wait. Photograph it first. If the item has a hole, don't repair it first. If a tag is missing, don't throw away the remaining packaging. If an accessory is missing, photograph the complete returned package and contents.

Evidence first. Fixing the item second.

How much does a damaged Poshmark return actually cost?

This is where the seller conversation usually stops too early. The original sale price is not automatically your loss. A useful way to think about the economic damage is:

What about the Poshmark selling fee?

Poshmark's current U.S. seller fee is 20% on sales of $15 or more and $2.95 on sales under $15. But don't automatically count the original 20% commission as a damaged-return loss. First reconcile what happened to the transaction and what Poshmark actually credited or charged. The important seller calculation is the loss that remains after the transaction itself has been reconciled. That is why the sale price alone is not enough to calculate the damage.

The three return scenarios sellers should never mix together

Scenario 1: Buyer returns the item in the same condition

A normal approved return. The economic problem is primarily the reversed transaction and the time spent handling it.

Scenario 2: Buyer returns the item damaged

The core subject of this page. Your evidence should establish: what condition the item was in before shipment → what condition it is in now.

Scenario 3: Buyer returns a different or incomplete item

A different evidence problem. You need to establish: what item you shipped → what item/package you received. A damaged item, a missing accessory, a wrong item and shipping damage should not all be treated as the same dispute.

A better way to think about Poshmark damaged returns

Think of every disputed return as having three snapshots:

SNAPSHOT 1 — BEFORE SHIPMENT: What did I actually send?→SNAPSHOT 2 — BUYER'S CLAIM: What does the buyer say is wrong?→SNAPSHOT 3 — RETURNED PACKAGE: What did I actually receive back?

Your job is to make those three snapshots easy to compare. That's much more useful than sending Poshmark a long paragraph explaining how frustrated you are.

What not to do when a return goes bad

  • Don't immediately accuse the buyer of fraud. Your evidence is more useful than the accusation.
  • Don't rely only on the original listing. Fresh pre-shipment photographs establish condition immediately before shipment.
  • Don't throw away the packaging — not until you've documented it.
  • Don't repair or clean the item before photographing it. You may destroy evidence of the condition in which it arrived.
  • Don't calculate your loss from the original sale price alone. The relevant question is what the item was worth before the return versus after, plus unrecovered seller costs.
  • Don't assume every return problem is the same. Damaged item, missing accessory, wrong item and shipping damage are different evidence problems.

Calculate the loss instead of guessing

If the returned item is worth less than it was before shipment, record the difference. For example: before $75 estimated resale value, after $35 estimated resale value, value reduction $40. Then add any other documented seller costs that actually remain unrecovered. That's the number worth tracking — and the mini calculator above works through it as an itemized number rather than a guess from the original sale price.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a buyer have to open a Poshmark return case?

Under Poshmark's current Posh Protect policy, a buyer has 72 hours from delivery — measured using the tracking information on the shipping label — to open a return case with supporting photos. If approved, Poshmark provides the return label and the buyer must ship within five days of approval.

Does Poshmark allow returns for fit or change of mind?

Generally no. Posh Protect covers specific problems such as undisclosed damage, an incorrect or missing item, an item not as described, or an authenticity issue — not ordinary buyer's remorse or fit issues.

What should I do if a Poshmark return arrives damaged?

Document before you clean, repair or wash anything. Photograph the package, the shipping label, the packaging condition, the opening if appropriate, the entire returned item and every discrepancy, then report the discrepancy through Poshmark's support process.

What if the returned item is not the item I shipped?

Build an evidence package: original listing photos, fresh pre-shipment photos, shipping photos connecting item to package, tags and labels, accessories and packaging. Compare identifying details and report the discrepancy through Poshmark's support process.

Who is liable if damage happened during return shipping?

It depends on the cause. Poshmark's policy says sellers are responsible for proper packaging and that Poshmark is not liable for damage caused by improper or inadequate seller packaging, so photograph the packaging before opening it to establish what arrived.

Does Poshmark refund the selling fee on an approved return?

Reconcile your transaction first. The important seller calculation is the loss that remains after the transaction itself has been reconciled — do not automatically count the original 20% or $2.95 fee as a damaged-return loss without checking what Poshmark actually credited or charged.

How do I calculate the loss on a damaged Poshmark return?

Return loss = change in the item's resale value + unrecovered transaction costs + other documented seller costs. Track the difference between what the item was worth before shipment and what it is worth after the return, plus any unrecovered seller costs.

Should I clean or repair a damaged return before photographing it?

No. Evidence first, fixing second. Cleaning, repairing or discarding packaging before photographing can destroy evidence of the condition in which the item arrived.

The seller rule worth remembering

Photograph before you ship. Photograph when the return arrives. Compare the two.

If the item comes back worse, the most useful question isn't "Why would a buyer do this?" It's: "What evidence can I show that proves the condition changed?" And once the evidence is documented, calculate the actual change in value. That is the part of a damaged Poshmark return that the normal transaction screen doesn't tell you.
