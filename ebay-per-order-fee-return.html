Does eBay Refund the Per-Order Fee on a Return? 2026 Seller Guide

Short answer: sometimes. If you're looking at an eBay refund and wondering, "Why did I get some of my fees back but not the $0.30/$0.40?", the answer depends on why the buyer returned the item and how the refund was handled.

For some eligible full refunds, eBay credits the per-order fixed amount along with the eligible final value fee. But for seller-responsibility returns, partial refunds, certain deductions, and cases where eBay steps in, the per-order fee is not credited.

So don't use the old rule of thumb that says "eBay never refunds the per-order fee." That's too broad for the current policy. Here's the useful version: the return reason matters.

The eBay per-order fee in plain English

eBay's final value fee has two pieces:

  1. a percentage-based final value fee, and
  2. a fixed per-order fee.

For orders of $10 or less, the per-order fee is $0.30. For orders over $10, it is $0.40. eBay says the total amount of the sale used for the percentage fee can include the item price, buyer-paid shipping, sales tax and other applicable amounts.

That fixed fee is easy to miss because it is tiny compared with the percentage fee. But when you're trying to reconcile a refund, that tiny line can be exactly what makes the numbers look wrong.

When does eBay refund the per-order fee?

A refund does not automatically mean every fee comes back. eBay has different rules for different refund situations.

Important: "Credited" doesn't necessarily mean you see the credit instantly. eBay says credits for the per-order fixed amount can take up to 10 days to appear in your transaction history.

The easiest example: buyer changes their mind

Imagine you sell a jacket for $60. The buyer receives it and opens a return because: "Doesn't fit."

You receive the item back and issue the eligible full refund through eBay. In this type of buyer-requested return, eBay's current policy says the eligible final value fee and the per-order fixed amount can be credited.

So this is not the situation where you should automatically assume the $0.40 is gone. That's an important correction to a lot of older seller explanations of eBay returns.

But an INAD return is different

Now change just one thing. The buyer says: "Item doesn't match the description."

That's a seller-responsibility return category. If you fully refund the buyer for one of the seller-responsibility reasons listed by eBay — including damaged, defective, wrong item, missing parts, or not matching the description/photos — eBay says it credits the eligible variable final value fee, but does not credit the per-order fixed amount.

So on a $100 order, the $0.40 can remain part of your cost. And that's where sellers often look at the transaction and think:

"Wait. I refunded the entire order. Why didn't everything reverse?"

Because "full refund" and "full fee credit" aren't necessarily the same thing.

What about a partial refund?

This is another place where the $0.40 gets confusing. Suppose:

  • Original order total: $68.05
  • Buyer receives a partial refund: $5.49
  • The refund represents about 8.1% of the original order

eBay's own example shows that the eligible percentage-based final value fee is credited proportionally — but the $0.40 per-order fee is not eligible for credit on that partial refund.

Partial refund ≠ 8.1% of every fee. The percentage-based eligible fee can be prorated. The fixed per-order amount isn't. This is also why the CLAWBACK eBay calculator treats the per-order fee separately from the proportional fee calculation.

Why does my eBay refund look different from the amount I originally paid?

This is probably one of the most useful questions to answer because sellers aren't usually looking at policy language when they're confused. They're looking at a transaction record.

You might see something like:

Original sale: $100.00→Final value fee: -$13.60→Per-order fee: -$0.40

Then the buyer receives a full refund. If the return qualifies for the appropriate fee credits, the percentage-based fee may be credited and, depending on the return situation, the per-order amount may also be credited. But if the return falls into a category where the fixed amount isn't eligible, that $0.40 doesn't come back.

That's why it's better to look at the transaction-level fee credit, rather than assuming: "I refunded $100, therefore all $14 disappeared and came back."

What if eBay steps in?

If eBay steps in to help with a return or item-not-received request and the case ends with a full or partial refund to the buyer, eBay says that situation does not qualify for seller fee credits under its listed exclusions.

So if you are reconciling a case where eBay took over, don't use the normal voluntary-refund calculation. That's a different bucket.

What if I refunded the buyer outside eBay?

Also different. If you send the buyer money outside eBay — for example, through another payment service — eBay says you aren't eligible for the fee credits covered by this policy. How the refund was processed matters.

What if the buyer returned the item damaged?

eBay says fee credits aren't issued when you deduct part of the buyer's refund because the returned item was used or damaged, subject to the eligibility rules around those deductions.

That means a seller shouldn't look at the deduction amount alone and assume the normal fee-credit calculation applies. If you're dealing with a damaged return, keep the transaction record and the evidence together:

  • original listing photos & item description
  • order details & return request
  • return tracking & photos/video of the returned item
  • messages with the buyer & refund calculation
  • eBay's case/decision record

What about cancellations?

Don't mix cancellations and returns when you're calculating the fee. eBay's current policy says an eligible cancellation for a reason outside the seller's control — such as the buyer requesting cancellation or a problem with the buyer's shipping address — can receive the per-order fixed amount credit.

But a seller-controlled cancellation, such as an item being out of stock or damaged, does not receive that per-order fee credit.

The important distinction isn't simply return vs cancellation. It's: What happened, why did it happen, and which eBay workflow was used?

How much is the eBay per-order fee?

For eBay.com, the current basic fee structure says:

  • $0.30 for orders of $10 or less
  • $0.40 for orders over $10

The percentage final value fee varies substantially by category, so don't treat 13.6% as a universal eBay rate. eBay's current fee table lists different rates for categories such as books, coins, handbags, jewelry, athletic shoes and others. That's why this article focuses on the fixed per-order fee, rather than pretending one percentage applies to every eBay sale.

What should I check if eBay didn't credit my fee?

Before contacting eBay, check these five things:

  1. What was the buyer's return reason? Don't rely on what the buyer told you in a message. Look at the actual return/request record.
  2. Was it a full or partial refund? Partial refunds use a different fee-credit calculation.
  3. Did eBay step in? If eBay intervened, the fee-credit rules are different.
  4. Did you issue the refund through eBay? An outside payment doesn't qualify for the listed fee credits.
  5. Has enough time passed? eBay says per-order fee credits can take up to 10 days to appear in transaction history.

One important correction to older eBay articles

You'll find older posts and seller discussions saying: "eBay never refunds the $0.30/$0.40 per-order fee."

That explanation is outdated as a blanket rule. Current eBay documentation specifically lists eligible full refunds where the per-order fixed amount is included in the fee credits, while separately listing situations where it isn't credited.

Seller discussions also show why this remains confusing in practice: sellers often describe the fixed fee, final value fee and promotional fees separately when reconciling refunds. Those community experiences are useful for identifying confusion, but eBay's current fee-credit documentation should be the source of truth for the actual rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does eBay refund the per-order fee on a return?

Sometimes. For eligible full refunds involving certain buyer-requested return reasons, eBay lists the per-order fixed amount among the fees that can be credited. For seller-responsibility returns, partial refunds and several other situations, it isn't credited.

Is the eBay per-order fee $0.30 or $0.40?

It's $0.30 for orders of $10 or less and $0.40 for orders over $10 under eBay's current basic fee structure. Some categories have different overall fee structures, so check your category as well.

Does eBay refund the per-order fee for buyer's remorse?

For eligible full refunds using the applicable eBay refund process, eBay lists buyer-requested reasons such as changed mind, doesn't fit, ordered by mistake and found a better price among situations where the per-order fixed amount can be credited.

Does eBay refund the per-order fee for INAD returns?

No, under the current fee-credit policy. For seller-responsibility reasons such as an item not matching the description/photos, eBay lists the variable final value fee as eligible but says the per-order fixed amount isn't credited.

Does eBay refund the per-order fee on a partial refund?

No. eBay's partial-refund examples show proportional credits for eligible percentage-based fees, while the per-order fixed amount is not eligible for credit.

How long does an eBay fee credit take?

eBay says credits for the per-order fixed amount can take up to 10 days to appear in your transaction history.

What happens to the final value fee on a partial refund?

For eligible partial refunds, eBay says fee credits are proportional to the amount refunded. Its published example calculates the refund as a percentage of the original total sale and applies that percentage to eligible fees.

What if eBay stepped in and refunded the buyer?

eBay lists cases where it stepped in and the buyer received a full or partial refund among situations that don't qualify for seller fee credits.

What if I refunded the buyer outside eBay?

eBay says refunds issued outside eBay don't qualify for the fee credits described in its policy.

Does the per-order fee apply to every eBay category?

The basic fee structure uses the $0.30/$0.40 per-order fee, but eBay has category-specific fee exceptions. For example, some athletic-shoe sales at qualifying prices have no per-order fee. Always check the fee table for your category.

What did your return actually cost you?

If you're reading this because you have a real returned order in front of you, this is the part that matters. Don't stop at "eBay refunded the buyer."
