Does eBay Refund the Per-Order Fee on a Return? (2026 Answer: No)

Short answer: no. When you refund a buyer, eBay credits the final value fee (13.6% in most categories) on the refunded amount — but the per-order fee stays gone. It's $0.30 for orders $10 or less and $0.40 for orders over $10, and eBay only credits it on cancellations, not returns. Here's exactly what you keep losing, when you CAN get it back, and how this compares to every other platform.

The one-liner: eBay charges the per-order fee per order, not per dollar. A refund doesn't cancel the order — it just reverses the sale. So the fee stays with eBay. The final value fee is percentage-based and comes back proportionally. The per-order fee is flat and doesn't.

The Two eBay Fees, And What Happens On A Refund

eBay charges two things on most sales: a final value fee (a percentage of the total sale amount including shipping and tax) and a small per-order fee. When you refund a buyer, eBay credits back the final-value-fee portion tied to the refund. The per-order fee is not refunded — it's charged per order, not per dollar, so a return doesn't cancel it.

Worked Example: The $0.40 That Never Comes Back

Say you sold an item for $60, the buyer opened a return claiming "doesn't fit," and everything else went smoothly. eBay refunds the buyer and credits the final value fee — but the $0.40 per-order fee is kept. Result: even a "perfect" return costs you $0.40. Now add return shipping you paid ($8.50 average) and any drop in the item's value, and that's your real loss.

The math that matters: 100 returns per year × $0.40 = $40 gone in per-order fees alone. 500 returns = $200. Scale that to a high-volume seller doing 2,000 returns annually, and you're looking at $800 evaporating from a fee most sellers don't even know exists. This is before return shipping, value drop, and the potential 5% Very High INAD penalty.

When DOES eBay Refund The Per-Order Fee? (Cancellations)

Here's the exception most sellers miss: the per-order fee IS credited on cancellations. If you cancel an order before it ships — whether the buyer requested it, you're out of stock, or there's a problem with the address — eBay credits both the final value fee and the per-order fee. The distinction is that a cancellation means the order never completed, while a return means the order completed and was then reversed.

This creates a weird incentive: if you know a sale is going to be returned (buyer messages "I ordered the wrong size"), you might be better off cancelling and relisting than letting it ship and come back. The per-order fee comes back on the cancellation. Of course, repeated cancellations hurt your seller metrics, so this isn't a strategy — just a fact worth knowing.

Confession: I didn't know about the cancellation vs return distinction for the first year I sold on eBay. I let dozens of "wrong size" orders ship and come back, losing $0.40 each time. Once I started cancelling and relisting when buyers admitted the mistake pre-shipment, I saved maybe $15/month. Not huge, but it's free money I was leaving on the table.

Does The Per-Order Fee Apply To Store Subscribers?

Yes. eBay Store subscribers (Starter, Basic, Premium, Anchor, Enterprise) still pay the per-order fee on every order, and it is still not refunded on returns. Store subscriptions lower the final value fee percentage (from 13.6% down to 12.7% or lower depending on tier and category) but do not eliminate the per-order fee. So even if you're paying $21.95/month for a Basic Store, you're still losing $0.40 on every return.

The Tax Angle: This Fee Is Deductible

Here's the silver lining: the per-order fee you paid on a returned sale is an ordinary business expense and is deductible on Schedule C. You paid it, it wasn't refunded, and it was a cost of doing business. Track it with your return records — one line per return showing the per-order fee lost — and you can deduct it at year-end.

The problem: most sellers never track it because they don't know it exists. They see the final value fee credit and assume "the fees came back." They don't realize $0.40 stayed with eBay. Over 100 returns, that's $40 of deductible expenses you never claimed. We explain the full tax write-off process in reseller tax write-offs.

The Real Loss: Per-Order Fee Is Just The Tip

The per-order fee is the smallest leak on a return, but it's the one that never comes back. The bigger leaks are:

  • Return shipping you paid: On "not as described" returns, eBay generates a label and deducts the cost from your funds. Average cost: $8-12 depending on weight and distance.
  • Value drop: The item comes back worn, stained, or damaged. A $60 dress that sold in December comes back in January with a makeup mark on the collar — now worth $25. That's $35 lost.
  • Very High INAD penalty: If your Item Not As Described rate is evaluated as "Very High" on the 20th of the month, eBay charges you an additional 5% on final value fees for sales in affected categories the following month. Four consecutive months bumps it to 6%.
  • Time: Processing the return, repacking the item, relisting it. This is opportunity cost you can't get back.

How To Track Your Per-Order Fee Loss

eBay doesn't give you a clean report of "per-order fees lost on returns." You have to track it manually:

  1. Go to My eBay > Orders > Sold
  2. For each returned order, check the transaction details
  3. Note the per-order fee that was charged and not credited
  4. Add it to a running total in a spreadsheet or use the CLAWBACK calculator to generate a "copy for taxes" line

At year-end, you'll have the exact number to deduct. Most sellers skip this step because it's tedious. The calculator does it for you — one line per return, timestamped, ready for your tax professional.

FAQ

Does eBay refund the per-order fee on a return?
No. The final value fee is credited on the refunded amount, but the per-order fee is not refunded on returns. It is only credited on cancellations.
Is it $0.30 or $0.40?
For orders $10.00 or less the per-order fee is $0.30. For orders over $10.00 the per-order fee is $0.40. This applies to most categories.
Does eBay refund the per-order fee if I cancel an order?
Yes. If you cancel an order before it ships (buyer requested, out of stock, or problem with address), eBay credits both the final value fee and the per-order fee. The per-order fee is only kept on returns, not cancellations.
Why doesn't eBay refund the per-order fee on returns?
The per-order fee covers the transaction infrastructure for that specific order. Since the order existed and was processed, eBay considers the fee earned regardless of whether the sale completes or is returned.
How much do I lose in per-order fees across many returns?
At $0.40 per return, 100 returns in a year costs you $40 in non-refunded per-order fees. At 500 returns, that's $200. This compounds with return shipping and value drop.
Can I write off the non-refunded per-order fee on my taxes?
Yes. The per-order fee you paid on a returned sale is an ordinary business expense and is deductible on Schedule C. Track it with your return records.
Do other platforms keep fees on returns like eBay does?
Depop refunds all fees (selling + processing + boosting) on Depop Payments refunds. Poshmark reverses the 20% commission on approved returns. Mercari reverses the 10% selling fee. eBay is unique in keeping a per-order fee that other platforms don't have.
Does the per-order fee apply to eBay Store subscribers?
Yes. Store subscribers still pay the per-order fee on every order, and it is still not refunded on returns. Store subscriptions lower the final value fee percentage but do not eliminate the per-order fee.
