Does eBay Refund the Per-Order Fee on a Return?

Short answer: no. When you refund a buyer, eBay credits the final value fee on the refunded amount — but the per-order fee stays gone. Here's exactly what you keep losing.

The two eBay fees, and what happens on a refund

eBay charges two things on most sales: a final value fee (a percentage of the total) and a small per-order fee. When you refund a buyer, eBay credits back the final-value-fee portion tied to the refund. The per-order fee is not refunded — it's charged per order, not per dollar, so a return doesn't cancel it.

On a clean return that's the only leak. On a messier one (paid return shipping, damaged item) the leak gets much bigger.

Worked example

Say you sold an item for $60, the buyer opened a return, and everything else went smoothly. eBay refunds the buyer and credits the final value fee — but the $0.40 per-order fee is kept. Result: even a "perfect" return costs you $0.40. Now add return shipping you paid and any drop in the item's value, and that's your real loss.

FAQ

Does eBay refund the per-order fee on a return?
No. The final value fee is credited on the refunded amount, but the per-order fee is not refunded.
Is it $0.30 or $0.40?
It depends on the item price and category — commonly $0.30 under $10 and $0.40 at $10 and above.
Can I get it back?
Generally no; it's a per-order charge. The calculator shows you exactly how much of it you lose.
